What's New, 3 November 2026

A short read of what’s landed, what’s coming, and what matters for Gateshead’s economy. This edition is a day late, so it covers the nine days since the 26 October edition rather than seven, and that takes in the Budget on 28 October. Three things carry it. Gateshead has been allocated £1.91 million from the new Derelict Buildings Fund. The Fiscal Devolution Roadmap names the North East in the first wave of mayoral business rates retention zones and gives mayors a 20% share of retained business rates from 2027. And business rates for 2027/28 are now set, with more relief for retail, hospitality and leisure firms.

What Landed

28 October · MHCLG

AmberGateshead Is Allocated £1.91 Million From the Derelict Buildings Fund

What changed?
MHCLG published the place selection and allocations for the new Derelict Buildings Fund on 28 October 2026. The fund provides £125 million, of which £120 million goes directly to 60 councils, chosen as the areas with the highest employment deprivation on the Indices of Deprivation. Gateshead’s allocation is £1.91 million. Across the NEMSA area, County Durham receives £3.00 million, Sunderland £2.71 million and South Tyneside £1.43 million. The largest urban council in each area that already has a City Densification Fund was excluded, which is why Newcastle is not on the list. The interim guidance says the money is a mix of capital and revenue, to bring prominent long-term vacant or derelict buildings back into community or commercial use, and the fund runs from 2026 to 2030.
Why it matters?
This is money already allocated to Gateshead, with no bidding round. By the end of 2026 councils are expected to start community engagement, start a longlist of buildings and receive a memorandum of understanding with an initial revenue payment. A first progress update to MHCLG is due by April 2027, with the first delivery payment in spring 2027. MHCLG will publish detailed guidance on funding profiles and project selection later this year.
What should we do?
Regeneration colleagues will want to start the building longlist and the community engagement before the end of the year. The guidance asks councils to keep their role as accountable body separate from any interest they have as owner of a building in scope.
28 and 30 October · MHCLG

AmberBusiness Rates for 2027/28 Are Set, With More Relief for Retail, Hospitality and Leisure

What changed?
MHCLG’s business rates information letter 8/2026 sets out the Budget package and the multipliers for 2027/28. It was corrected on 30 October so that inflation is applied consistently to the retail, hospitality and leisure multipliers. The 2027/28 multipliers are 49.7p standard, 44.7p small business, 44.5p standard retail, hospitality and leisure, 39.5p small business retail, hospitality and leisure, and 52.5p for high-value properties. Pubs, social clubs and all but the largest live music venues get an extra 20% relief, taking their total to 35% in 2027/28 and 2028/29. Small Business Rates Relief thresholds for qualifying retail, hospitality and leisure properties rise to £18,000 for full relief and £21,000 for tapered relief. Bills for qualifying retail, hospitality and leisure properties, pubs and live music venues, of any size, are capped at CPI inflation in 2027/28 and 2028/29. Vape shops lose Small Business Rates Relief, and vape shops and adult gaming centres lose the retail, hospitality and leisure multipliers.
Why it matters?
The new thresholds are aimed at small retail, hospitality and leisure premises, the kind of business found across Gateshead’s town and district centres, and the bill cap covers qualifying premises of any size. Firms will not see the change until their April 2027 bills. Councils collect the rates, and the letter confirms the government will meet the reasonable costs of putting the changes in place.
What should we do?
Business support colleagues will want to tell retail, hospitality and leisure firms, pubs and live music venues what changes in April 2027. Business rates colleagues will need the technical annex in the letter.
28 October · HM Treasury

AmberThe North East Is in the First Wave of Mayoral Business Rates Retention Zones

What changed?
HM Treasury published the Fiscal Devolution Roadmap with the Budget. It says local and regional government will retain 100% of business rates income before the end of the decade, in place of grants from central government, with councils keeping at least 70%. The first step is a 20% share of locally retained business rates for mayoral strategic authorities from 2027, and the roadmap says further detail comes through the Local Government Finance Settlement later this year. The Budget document (paragraph 4.61) names the North East among the first wave of new mayoral Business Rates Retention Zones, subject to full business case approval and legislation. The roadmap says those zones are protected from business rates resets for 25 years. Fiscal devolution baselines will be set at the spending review in 2027.
Why it matters?
The money replaces grants, so whether Gateshead gains turns on the baselines, the resets and how equalisation between places works, and none of those is fixed yet. A retention zone gives NEMSA a revenue stream inside a defined area, so where the zone is drawn matters for Gateshead sites.
What should we do?
Finance colleagues will want to watch the Local Government Finance Settlement later this year. Nothing for us until the settlement is published.
28 October · HM Treasury

AmberNational Design Rules for the Overnight Visitor Levy

What changed?
The Fiscal Devolution Roadmap sets the national framework for the Overnight Visitor Levy, which every strategic authority will be able to introduce. The levy will be a percentage of the accommodation cost, on short-term commercially let visitor accommodation, with national exemptions for non-commercial accommodation. Strategic authorities can choose to exempt types such as tent pitches, caravan pitches and hostels. They will collect the levy themselves, the accommodation provider is liable for it, and they must consult locally and give businesses advance notice. The roadmap says legislation will be introduced shortly, and strategic authorities are expected to set out plans for spending the revenue by March 2028.
Why it matters?
NEMSA would decide whether to introduce a levy, and Gateshead’s hotels and short-term lets would collect it if it did. The roadmap keeps the decision and the revenue with the strategic authority, so Gateshead’s say runs through NEMSA. A rate set as a percentage of the room price raises less where room prices are lower.
What should we do?
Visitor economy colleagues will want to note the design rules ahead of any NEMSA consultation. The ONS release on short-term lets through online platforms is due at 9:30am on 13 November.
28 October · MHCLG

AmberA New High Streets Strategy, and Rental Auction Funding Opening the Week of 23 November

What changed?
MHCLG published Hope on the High Street, the high streets strategy for England, backed by a £210 million Community Assets Programme. The Derelict Buildings Fund is the largest part. The High Street Rental Auctions Fund prospectus offers up to £10 million to help councils use their power to auction the lease of a commercial property that has been empty for 366 days or more in 24 months. Applications open in the week starting 23 November 2026 and are assessed on a rolling basis, with early applications prioritised, until 31 January 2028 or until the money runs out. The strategy names Gateshead once, as a case study of brownfield funding for Capital and Centric’s redevelopment of the Old Town Hall and Civic Quarter, including 250 new homes.
Why it matters?
The rental auctions fund pays for the groundwork councils need, such as vacancy registers, property surveys and refurbishment to a minimum letting standard. Because early applications are prioritised, the date it opens matters more than the closing date.
What should we do?
Regeneration colleagues will want to decide before 23 November whether Gateshead applies, and in which centres.
28 October and 2 November · HM Treasury, BIST

AmberA Northern Growth Response, and a Search for Up to 10 Strategic Sites That Includes the North East

What changed?
At the Budget HM Treasury published its Summary of Responses and Government Response on the Northern Growth Strategy, from 131 substantive responses. The March 2026 Next Steps document on the same page already set out £120 million of new grant for the North East through NEMSA, working with Newcastle City Council and Gateshead Council, with projects in a proposed Mayoral Development Zone spanning Newcastle and Gateshead and Gateshead Quays and Forth Yards as flagship anchors. On 2 November the government launched the Strategic Sites Accelerator, around £700 million to help unlock up to 10 major industrial and commercial sites across the UK. The announcement says it is exploring investment opportunities in eight areas with National Wealth Fund strategic partnerships, including the North East, and that it is open to sites in every region. The Budget document also says the next International Investment Summit will be in Newcastle in spring 2027.
Why it matters?
Gateshead Quays sits inside two of these offers, the £120 million North East grant and the proposed Mayoral Development Zone. The accelerator is aimed at commercially viable sites held back by finance, grid connections or planning.
What should we do?
Economic development colleagues will want to know about the accelerator, which works through mayors and local partners. Nothing for us.
28 October · HM Treasury

AmberThe Green Book Is Updated, With a 3.00% Discount Rate

What changed?
HM Treasury updated the 2026 edition of The Green Book on 28 October 2026 to reflect the new approach to discounting, with its response to the discount rate review. The discount rate for the first 30 years of a proposal falls from 3.50% to 3.00%, years 31 to 75 from 3.00% to 2.50%, and years 76 to 125 from 2.50% to 2.25%. The separate 1.50% health discount rate is no longer recommended. New guidance on developing place-based business cases and on proportionate business cases came out the same day, along with Supporting local investment, an offer to places, which sets out the support public investment bodies offer local areas.
Why it matters?
Every business case Gateshead or NEMSA puts to government is appraised against the Green Book. A lower discount rate gives more weight to benefits that arrive years after the money is spent, which helps regeneration and skills schemes whose returns are slow. A place-based case lets a group of linked projects be appraised together.
What should we do?
Anyone preparing a business case from now on should use the updated 2026 edition and the new discount rates.
28 October · MHCLG, HM Treasury

AmberA £100 Household Energy Payment, Paid Through Councils From January

What changed?
The Budget announced a £2.6 billion Household Energy Payment of £100 for households in England in council tax bands A to D. MHCLG’s council tax information letter 6/2026 says it is a one-off payment for this financial year, aimed at properties that are someone’s sole or main residence. Councils are expected to start paying from January 2027, with funding in advance and a reconciliation afterwards. A £100 million discretionary fund lets councils help other households hit by energy bills, including some in higher bands. Detailed guidance is to follow shortly.
Why it matters?
Gateshead has 91,470 properties in council tax bands A to D out of 95,630 (VOA, Council Tax stock of properties, at 9 September 2025), which is approximately 96% of the stock against approximately 81% in England. Not every property will qualify, because second homes and empty homes fall outside the core scheme, but it suggests the payment reaches a larger share of homes here than in most places, and Gateshead Council does the paying.
What should we do?
Revenues colleagues will want the delivery guidance as soon as it is published, given payments start in January.
29 October · DWP

AmberDWP Estimates the North East Has the Highest NEET Rate of England’s Regions

What changed?
DWP’s Get Britain Working, Labour Market Insights for October 2026 puts the North East’s NEET rate for those aged 16 to 24 for the year ending Apr-26 to Jun-26 at 20.3% (±3.7pp), against England at 13.2% (±0.7pp) (Labour Force Survey). DWP estimates this is the highest of England’s regions, and says there is some uncertainty in the data. The same release gives the monthly into-work rate for Universal Credit claimants in the ‘Searching for work’ group by council. Gateshead’s for Jun-26 is 7.1%, against 7.3% for Great Britain. These are official statistics in development.
Why it matters?
The North East’s rate is significantly above England’s, because the two intervals do not overlap. Its interval does overlap those of Yorkshire and the Humber and the East Midlands, so we cannot say for certain that it is the highest region. It is a regional figure, so it cannot say where Gateshead sits within the region, and the Labour Force Survey behind it is no longer accredited. The into-work rate is an administrative measure, with no sampling interval, and it moves a lot from month to month.
What should we do?
Employment and skills colleagues will want to know. The next regional figure is in DWP’s next edition, due on 28 January 2027.

28 October · HM Treasury

GreenThe Trading Allowance Triples to £3,000 From April 2027

What changed?
The Budget document triples the Trading Allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, to help people take the first steps in starting a small business. HM Treasury also published Entrepreneurship in the UK, Start. Scale. Stay., and opened a technical consultation on Founders’ Relief (closes 30 November) and a consultation on corporate tax simplification for business investment (closes 21 November). 29 October · ONS

GreenGateshead Recorded 155 Business Creations and 130 Closures in Jul to Sep 2026

What changed?
Gateshead’s count of business creations for Jul-26 to Sep-26 is 155, and its count of business closures is 130 (ONS, Business demography, quarterly, low-level geographic breakdown, UK, 29 October 2026). A year earlier, in Jul-25 to Sep-25, they were 170 and 135. Over the four quarters to Sep-26 there were approximately 710 creations and 635 closures, against approximately 665 of each in the four quarters before. Across the UK, creations fell 3.5% on a year and closures 1.1%. The counts are rounded to the nearest 5 and are official statistics in development.
Why it matters?
On these early figures more businesses started than closed in Gateshead over the last year, where the year before they balanced. ONS adjusts these early counts for later changes to the business register, so they are an early indication only. The annual Business Demography release is provisionally due on 19 November.
What should we do?
Nothing for us.

GreenGovernment Minded to Proceed With Reorganisation in Five Areas, With the Rest Decided by Early 2027

What changed?
MHCLG announced on 29 October 2026 that it is minded to go ahead with reorganisation in East Sussex and Brighton and Hove, Gloucestershire, Oxfordshire, Warwickshire and Worcestershire, with at least three weeks for further representations. Decisions for nine areas paused in September are withdrawn, and all 15 remaining areas will have an outcome by early next year. New councils will be set up for 2029, with shadow elections in May 2028. MHCLG published minded-to letters, review update letters and a collection page for each area.
Why it matters?
Gateshead is already a unitary council, so none of this changes its structure. It does change the map of English councils, and with it some of the comparator areas we benchmark against.
What should we do?
Nothing for us.
