Tue 15 Sep · ONS and NOMIS · new local figure

RedThe claimant count rose 210 in August, and it is the biggest August rise in eleven years

What changed?
Gateshead's claimant count for August 2026 is 5,100, which is 4.1% of residents aged 16-64 (NOMIS, Claimant count by sex and age, NM_162_1, not seasonally adjusted). That is up 210 on July 2026 and up 100 on August 2025. The North East is 63,800 and 3.7%, England is 1,486,460 and 4.0%, on the same pull. The borough is above both. Inside the borough the spread is wide, from 395 in Deckham to 75 in Low Fell, on May 2025 ward boundaries.
Why it matters?
August usually moves very little. We compared every July to August step since 2015, leaving out 2020 because the pandemic step makes it meaningless, and Gateshead's 4.3% rise this year is the largest of the eleven. The median of the other ten years is 0.9%, and in four of the last five years August fell. The same is true regionally and nationally, at 2.7% and 2.2% against medians of 0.5% and minus 0.1%, so the cause is very unlikely to be local. What is local is the size. Gateshead's step is about twice England's.
Two cautions travel with that. The Gateshead series is available unadjusted only, because the seasonally adjusted claimant count is published for national geographies, so a single month cannot be read as a turning point. And the proportion uses a NOMIS population denominator that still lags the mid-2025 estimates, which is a known and already recorded exposure.
What should we do?
Carry this into the Monthly Economic Update on 17 September as the lead, with the eleven-year comparison shown rather than asserted. If the September figure repeats it, the borough has a change in level and not a seasonal artefact, and that is a different conversation with colleagues.
Tue 15 Sep · NOMIS · new local figure

AmberThe 18 to 24 claimant count rose faster still, and it is the group the NEET work already flags

What changed?
Gateshead's claimant count for those aged 18-24 for August 2026 is 1,040, up 80 on July 2026 and up 100 on August 2025 (NOMIS, NM_162_1). The North East is 14,715 and England 295,410.
Why it matters?
The 8.3% monthly rise is again the largest July to August step in eleven years, against a median of 1.9% for the other ten. The regional and national steps are 6.6% and 5.0%, so young people are moving more than the whole cohort everywhere, which is what a summer of school and college leavers looks like. Gateshead is once more the largest of the three. This is the same 18 to 24 group our NEET work identifies as the borough's exposed cohort, where Gateshead holds up well at 16 to 17 and does not at 18 to 24.
What should we do?
Add August to the NEET thread's evidence rather than opening new work. The thread already carries offered follow-ups that have not been commissioned, and one month of an unadjusted series does not change what those follow-ups should be.
Tue 15 Sep · ONS · quarterly local refresh

GreenPayrolled employees fell in line with the UK, but Gateshead pay is growing more slowly

What changed?
The local authority sheets of the PAYE Real Time Information tables refreshed today, as scheduled, and now run to August 2026 (ONS, Earnings and employment from Pay As You Earn Real Time Information, seasonally adjusted reference tables, released 15 September 2026). Gateshead's payrolled employees for August 2026 is 86,302 and median monthly pay is £2,517, both seasonally adjusted and all industries. The workbook's own next update field reads 15 December 2026.
Why it matters?
On our own subtraction from the published figures, payrolled employees are down approximately 405 on August 2025, which is approximately 0.47%, against a UK fall of approximately 0.48%. Gateshead is doing what the country is doing. Pay is the part that differs. Median monthly pay is up approximately 2.90% on the year here against approximately 3.51% for the UK, so the monthly gap to the UK median has widened from £121 to £140 over twelve months.
Two administrative sources now point the same way for August, which is a stronger statement than either makes alone, and neither carries a confidence interval to qualify.
What should we do?
Nothing beyond the monthly brief. Any product keyed to the local authority sheets should be rebuilt now rather than in October, because the next local update is not until 15 December.
Wed 9 Sep · NatWest and S&P Global · business survey

GreenThe North East business activity index fell below the no-change mark

What changed?
The North East Business Activity Index for August 2026 is 49.6 (NatWest UK Regional Growth Tracker, August 2026, published under embargo to 00:01 on 9 September 2026). Northern Ireland is highest at 55.5 and Scotland lowest at 48.9. The release describes the four regions below 50 as each seeing slight decreases in activity. Responses were collected 12 to 26 August 2026, and the regions follow International Territorial Level 1 definitions, so this is the North East region.
Why it matters?
Below 50 means the businesses surveyed reported falling activity rather than growth. The June edition put the region at 50.8. We hold no primary-source July reading, so we are not drawing a month-on-month move.
What should we do?
Nothing this week. If September is a second month below 50, it belongs in the monthly brief beside the claimant count, where the survey supplies the turn and the claimant count supplies the level.

Policy and the Centre

Thu 10 Sep · MHCLG and HM Treasury · government response

RedThe visitor levy is now designed, and none of the decisions that matter are Gateshead's

What changed?
The government published its response to the visitor levy consultation, which ran from 26 November 2025 to 18 February 2026 and drew 1,223 responses (Visitor Levy in England, government response, 10 September 2026). The design is settled. The levy is a percentage of accommodation cost rather than a flat fee, there is no national cap, accommodation providers are liable on self-assessment, and strategic authorities become the tax authorities. All mayoral and foundation strategic authorities get the power, which answers one of our open watch questions. Local consultation and a final prospectus are required before any levy starts, and there is no national minimum notice period.
On timing, the response says the government intends to legislate during the current Parliamentary session and that, subject to Parliamentary approval, it expects local leaders to be able to set out plans for how revenues will be invested by March 2028. That settles the discrepancy we recorded on 8 September between a spring 2027 commencement and the Prime Minister's wording of towards the end of the financial year 2027-28. March 2028 is the spending-plan milestone, and a first charge sits later still behind consultation, a prospectus and a local notice period.
Why it matters?
Two decisions in the response remove options the borough might have assumed it had. A levy cannot be applied to part of a strategic authority's area, so a Quays-only or Newcastle-Gateshead-only levy is ruled out, and the rate cannot vary by constituent authority, so a Gateshead rate is not possible either. Revenue is invested by the strategic authority in line with regional growth strategies, and passing money to a constituent council happens only where the strategic authority decides to do so. Gateshead therefore has no automatic share.
What follows is that the borough's influence runs entirely through the evidence it puts into NEMSA's consultation, and through where revenue is spent rather than how the levy is charged. We hold no borough overnight-stay or accommodation-stock baseline, so today we have nothing to put in.
What should we do?
Start the baseline now. Overnight stays, accommodation stock by type, and the share of it that would fall inside and outside the national exemptions. That is the document the borough will need when the regional prospectus is drafted, and it takes longer to build than the consultation will run.
One thing to avoid. Regional press has reported a request from the Mayor for a 5% cap. We could not find a primary source for it, so it should not be quoted as NEMSA policy.
Mon 7 Sep, published Thu 10 Sep · Cabinet Office, HM Treasury and the Attorney General · centre of government

AmberMinisters have been told there is no general duty to consult, and the steer reaches the wider public sector

What changed?
A joint letter from the Chancellor of the Exchequer, the First Secretary of State and the Attorney General, dated 7 September 2026 and published on 10 September, sets out action on consultations, legal considerations and judicial review (The Simplification and Agency of Government). It states that the government will "end this trend by reasserting that there is no general duty of consultation, and that the default position should be that ministers reach decisions and proceed to act on them", and that formal consultation should happen only where there is a statutory requirement, where it would be conspicuously unfair not to consult, or where ministers judge it the right vehicle. It adds that the government wants "this approach to consultations adopted not just within central government and its agencies, but across the wider public sector". The accompanying release says an AI tool identified approximately 7,000 consultation duties in legislation for review, and promises new public participation guidance in autumn 2026 (Cabinet Office, 10 September 2026).
Why it matters?
Consultation responses are one of the main routes this team uses to put Gateshead evidence in front of government, and the supply of them is about to fall. The letter also puts statutory reporting requirements in scope for repeal, and some of what the council produces exists because of those duties. The line about the wider public sector is an explicit steer at councils' own practice, so it is the primary source to cite if the council's engagement approach is reviewed.
What should we do?
Treat "wait for the consultation" as a weaker plan than it was, and put evidence in earlier through direct channels. The high streets package below is the immediate test of whether that is already happening.
Mon 14 Sep · MHCLG · funding window open

RedCapital funding for community spaces is open, and it closes on 12 October

What changed?
Applications opened on 14 September for the Common Ground Award 2026 to 2027 (MHCLG prospectus, announcement, 14 September 2026). Up to £2.5 million is available, capital only, covering construction, renovation of facilities, spaces and equipment. Individual organisations can bid for £20,000 to £50,000, exceptionally up to £100,000, and consortiums for £100,000 to £250,000. Applications close on 12 October 2026, with decisions in December and payment expected in January 2027.
Why it matters?
It is capital money for community spaces on a four-week window, and the consortium band is large enough to matter for a place-based bid in a local centre. Eligibility sits with voluntary, community and social enterprise organisations rather than the council, so the council's role is convening, and convening takes most of the four weeks.
What should we do?
This needs a yes or no this week rather than a place on a list. If the answer is yes, the useful contribution from this team is the evidence on which local centre has the strongest case, which we can produce quickly from existing deprivation and local-centre work.

AmberConnect to Work has published outcomes, and a quarter of recent participants are the age group we are worried about

Ask NEMSA or DWP for the North East delivery-area breakdown rather than citing the England and Wales headline. If it arrives, it is a commissionable follow-up on the NEET thread rather than a new piece of work.
Checked Tue 15 Sep · MHCLG · no change, fifth week

GreenThe high streets package still has no consultation, five weeks after it was announced

What changed?
Nothing, which is the finding. The package announced on 11 August would take vape shops out of Use Class E into a standalone class, require planning permission for adult gaming centres, revoke the "aim to permit" duty in the Gambling Act and extend closure orders (gov.uk, 11 August 2026). MHCLG's open consultation list was checked again today and none of the six items on it is this package.
Why it matters?
Press coverage at announcement indicated a fast-tracked six-week consultation and first measures operating from the beginning of 2027. A six-week consultation opening after today cannot close before late October, and the secondary legislation has to follow, so that timetable is now visibly tight. The letter above tells departments there is no general duty to consult, which makes re-scoping or dropping the consultation a live possibility, though neither document says so and that reading is ours.
What should we do?
Stop treating an early 2027 commencement as a planning assumption in local-centre work. The baseline count of vape, betting and adult gaming hereditaments across the borough's local centres still needs doing before any use-class change removes the counterfactual, and it is now more urgent rather than less.

Corrections and method

GreenThree things about our own collection, two of them faults we found this week

What changed?
The commencement discrepancy recorded on 8 September is resolved. The visitor levy government response gives March 2028 for spending plans, which is consistent with the Prime Minister's wording of towards the end of the financial year 2027-28 and supersedes the spring 2027 we previously held.
The gov.uk organisation identifier we used for MHCLG returns nothing. The live identifier has no "and" in it, and a legacy identifier from the department's previous name still carries items, so both have to be queried or every MHCLG row silently disappears from the log. Building this week's log the first time produced zero MHCLG rows and no error.
The gov.uk search index is not complete on its own either. Two items appeared in the mailroom feed but not in the search results for the same window, so the log below is now the union of both sources rather than the search alone.
Why it matters?
The completeness rule for the departmental log only works if the query behind it is right. A wrong identifier fails silently and produces a log that reconciles perfectly against itself, which is the same failure mode as the mailroom truncation found on 8 September. Two independent sources checked against each other is the only test that catches it.
What should we do?
Record both MHCLG identifiers in the schedule specification, and keep building the log as the union of the search API and the feed. Both changes are worth making before the next edition rather than being rediscovered.

A week where the local numbers and the policy both moved, and in the same direction. The claimant count rose 210 to 5,100 in August and the rise is the largest August step in eleven years in Gateshead, in the North East and in England, with Gateshead's about twice England's. Payrolled employees fell in line with the UK while local pay grew 0.6 percentage points slower, so the borough is holding jobs and losing ground on earnings. The visitor levy became the first piece of the fiscal devolution package to move from announcement to settled design, and every decision it creates belongs to NEMSA rather than to the borough, which makes the evidence we can put into a regional consultation the only lever we have. At the centre, departments have been told there is no general duty to consult, which narrows that same route just as we come to depend on it. Two dates moved in our favour and one commitment slipped. Three corrections to our own collection are recorded above, two of them faults found this week. This is the first edition in the new format, with the dashboard, the significance grading and the top actions box replacing the long read, and the complete departmental log folded away rather than cut.
