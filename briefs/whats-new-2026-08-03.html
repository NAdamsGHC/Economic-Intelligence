What's New · weekly round-up · covering 28 July – 3 August

What's New — 3 August 2026

A short weekly read of what's coming, what's landed and what matters for Gateshead's economy. Each item says why it's here. A quiet week for headline statistics, but it carried two releases that change what we can measure and one date that fixes the autumn.

The one to act on. New mid-2025 population estimates landed on 29 July. They are the denominator under every per-head and per-resident rate we publish, so anything quoting a rate on a mid-2024 base is now on an old denominator. This affects the claimant rate, jobs density and GVA per head.

Landed this week

Wed 29 July

Population estimates for England and Wales, mid-2025

Why it matters: a new latest population vintage for Gateshead, down to local authority by single year of age and sex, and already loaded on NOMIS. Every rate we publish divides by this. The small-area and ward edition follows on 22 October. Worth a short piece on how much of the recent movement in our claimant rate is the numerator and how much is the denominator.

Wed 29 July

Workless households for regions across the UK, 2025

Why it matters: the title says regions, but the tables include local authority versions. This is the only local authority level measure of children in workless households, which is a genuine gap in what we can currently say. It connects the NEET work to household context. Survey-derived, so confidence intervals apply. Good candidate for the 20 August monthly brief.

Fri 31 July

Budget confirmed for Wednesday 28 October

Why it matters: this is the date the fiscal devolution detail arrives. The 30 July announcement gave mayors a share of income tax and 100% business rates retention, with mayors retaining revenue from spring 2027, but deferred every mechanism that decides whether this helps or hurts us. Retention-based systems reward buoyant tax bases and low service demand, and Gateshead has neither, so the equalisation design is the whole question. Our standing Budget watch now moves to reading the fiscal devolution roadmap and the devolution white paper in full on 29 October.

Thu 30 July

DWP, Get Britain Working: Labour Market Insights, July 2026

Why it matters: first publication of a new series covering into-work rates, employment of Universal Credit customers, and NEET rates for England's regions. It feeds the live NEET thread directly. Regional for NEET; whether the Universal Credit breakdowns go below region is not stated on the landing page and needs checking.

Fri 31 July

MHCLG, council tax and non-domestic rates collection rates, England 2025-26

Why it matters: billing authority level, so genuinely local, and it pairs with the non-domestic rates collected release from 22 July. A combined read on business rates reliefs and collection in Gateshead against North East comparators would serve the high streets work. Not commissioned.

Thu 30 July

Health state life expectancy estimates for MSOAs, England and Wales, 2019 to 2023

Why it matters: below local authority, which is unusual for health expectancy. Set against IoD2025 deprivation deciles it would give a defensible local reading of health-related economic inactivity, which we can currently only assert at regional level. Deep-dive candidate.

Fri 31 July

ONS, update on reinstating quarterly regional GDP estimates

Why it matters: mostly as something not to misread. Quarterly regional GDP is coming back for England and Wales, but at ITL1, which is the North East as a whole and not Gateshead. No timetable was given for lower geographies. Worth knowing before somebody reports it as a new local series.

Thu 30 July

The Productivity Institute, July output

Why it matters: two papers are on-theme. The Greater Manchester growth paper asks whether Greater Manchester is genuinely growing and why, across output, productivity and earnings. The Walsall paper frames a place-based investment strategy for a borough with a similar profile to ours, combining economic investment with skills, health and community development. Both are methodologically close to the Local Economic Assessment work. The call for papers for the September conference closed on 31 July, with decisions on 7 August.

Filtered out this week

Quarterly business demography for April to June 2026 (UK, industry and region only, so no Gateshead read; the local authority numbers still come from the annual edition in November); estimates of the very old including centenarians (ageing context, no economic read); population estimates by marital status and living arrangements (England and Wales national); the Community Life Survey and Community and Engagement Survey topic reports (no local authority breakdown evidenced, though worth a one-off look for the visitor economy work); the diffusion indices comparison paper (method); DCMS heritage sector methodology; Companies House incorporation statistics for April to June 2026 (UK register level); UKRI funding notices, which are restricted to research organisations; Bank of England credit union statistics; and the usual run of FCA and financial regulation alerts. All tracked, none actioned.

Standing watch, now closed on our side. We have the 20 July ONS Economic Forum slidepack. ONS still has not posted it publicly, with the SlideShare account's most recent Economic Forum item remaining the 18 May edition, so the public gap is real, but we no longer need to wait on it. Three things in it are worth knowing. The independent forecasts HM Treasury collates put CPI back at 3.2% by the fourth quarter of 2026, so the current easing is a trough rather than a trend. AI use among businesses with 10 or more employees has almost tripled since late 2023, reaching 34.8% by March 2026, and it varies from 58% in information and communication down to 13% in construction. And ONS will publish in August its assessment of readiness to move to the Transformed Labour Force Survey as the headline labour market measure in 2027, which is the survey sitting behind every local estimate we caveat so heavily.
