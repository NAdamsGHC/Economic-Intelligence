What's New, 19 October 2026

A short read of what’s landed, what’s coming, and what matters for Gateshead’s economy. This edition covers the six days since the 14 October edition. Three things carry it. A smaller share of Gateshead pupils sustained a place in education, an apprenticeship or work after their GCSEs, and the gap to England widened. The government opened its consultation on employment status and the self-employed, which closes on 8 January. And the Pathways to Work Innovation Fund closes in a week, with still no partner from the NEMSA area in its directory.

16 October · DWP · carried from 28 September

RedThe Innovation Fund Closes in a Week, and the Directory Still Names No Partner From the NEMSA Area

What changed?
DWP updated the partnership directory in the applicant bid pack again on 16 October 2026. It now lists 255 entries, 237 of them organisations, up from 245 on 13 October. None names any of the seven NEMSA authorities or a place within them. The only entries naming a North East place are still the two in Tees Valley, a joint entry from Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council and Teesside University and one from First Stop Darlington. The page still says an online application portal will launch in early October, and applications can go by post or email in the meantime. Applications close on 26 October and shortlisting outcomes follow on 9 November.
Why it matters?
Economic inactivity driven by long-term sickness is one of the structural weaknesses in Gateshead’s labour market, and this money is aimed at it. The item has carried for three weeks with no decision recorded, and the next edition falls on the closing day.
What should we do?
Settle this week whether Gateshead bids, joins a bid or passes. If Gateshead is involved, the evidence case on inactivity and health is ours to supply.

16 October · ONS · carried from 15 September

RedONS Publishes a Year of North East Overseas Visits on Its New Survey Design

What changed?
ONS published Travel trends: 2025 on 16 October 2026, with quarterly estimates by region in its overseas residents dataset. The North East’s number of visits by overseas residents for July to September 2025 is 169,000, the busiest of the four quarters. Over the four quarters of 2025 that comes to approximately 504,000 visits, 4.7 million nights and £314 million of spending (our sum of the published quarters). These are official statistics in development. ONS says some regional estimates rest on small samples, publishes no confidence intervals for them, and advises against comparing them with estimates from before July 2024, because the survey design changed from July to September 2024. Our accommodation-stock baseline has not been started.
Why it matters?
This is the first full year of regional overseas figures on the new design, so it can stand as the overseas part of a levy baseline. It is regional only and says nothing about Gateshead directly, and overseas visitors are a minority of overnight stays.
What should we do?
Start the overnight-stay and accommodation-stock baseline from the sources available now, using these figures for the overseas part and labelling them as regional. Add the ONS short-term lets release, provisionally due on 13 November, and the national register when it exists.
14 October · BIST

AmberConsultation on Employment Status and the Self-Employed Closes on 8 January

What changed?
BIST opened its consultation on employment status reform and protections for the self-employed on 14 October 2026. It proposes placing less weight on personal service when deciding whether someone is a worker, a presumption of worker status with stronger enforcement, and new protections for the genuinely self-employed, including written statements, protection from blacklisting and health and safety cover. It closes at 11:55pm on 8 January 2027. BIST’s announcement says external estimates suggest as many as 900,000 people may be wrongly treated as self-employed, and that the plans would help more of them get the minimum wage and holiday pay. This is the consultation that was still undated in our last two editions.
Why it matters?
Where people are wrongly classed as self-employed they miss the minimum wage and holiday pay, so the reform bears on low pay and in-work earnings in the borough. Self-employment is a thread we have written on before, and the consultation asks for evidence on how the proposals would work in practice.
What should we do?
Decide by early December whether Gateshead responds. A short evidence note on self-employment in the borough, refreshed from the latest data, is a proposal for commissioning.
15 October · DfE

AmberA Smaller Share of Gateshead Pupils Sustained a Destination After Key Stage 4

What changed?
Gateshead’s percentage of pupils sustaining an education, apprenticeship or employment destination after key stage 4, for pupils in state-funded mainstream and special schools who finished key stage 4 in 2023/24, is 89.7% (1,863 of 2,078 pupils) (DfE, Key stage 4 destination measures, academic year 2024/25, published 15 October 2026). For pupils who finished a year earlier it was 91.3%. The North East is at 89.3% (89.2% a year earlier) and England at 91.3% (91.6%). The share of Gateshead pupils whose activity was not captured in the data rose from 2.3% to 3.4%. These are administrative records for the whole cohort, so they carry no sampling interval.
Why it matters?
Our NEET work has read Gateshead as relatively strong at 16 and 17 and exposed at 18 to 24. A year ago the borough was within half a point of England on this measure. It is now 1.6 percentage points below England and close to the North East average. At the previous year’s rate, approximately 35 more young people from this cohort would have sustained a destination (our calculation, 91.3% of 2,078 against 1,863). One year does not make a trend, and part of the fall is the rise in pupils whose activity was not captured.
What should we do?
Fold this into the NEET thread and check it against the ONS NEET release on 26 November before it is used in a product. A breakdown by disadvantage and by special educational needs is published at local authority level and is a proposal for commissioning.

14 and 15 October · ONS

GreenGDP Grew by 0.1% in August, and the Replacement for the Annual Population Survey Stays on a Data-Led Timetable

What changed?
The UK’s monthly real GDP growth for August 2026 is 0.1%, following 0.4% in July (ONS, GDP monthly estimate, UK: August 2026, 15 October 2026). Over the three months to August, GDP grew by 0.7%, the eighth three-month period of growth in a row. Production grew by 0.8% in August, services were flat and construction fell by 0.2%. On 14 October the ONS published its quarterly improvement plan update. It says the Transformed Labour Force Survey will replace the Annual Population Survey, that the move remains a data-led decision for 2027, and that a progress check in January 2027 will lead to a transition plan published early in 2027. The parallel running of the two surveys into 2027 has led the ONS to scale back interviewer recruitment.
Why it matters?
GDP is national context with no local breakdown. The survey change matters more to us, because the Annual Population Survey is the source of every local employment, unemployment and inactivity rate we publish.
What should we do?
Nothing now. The January progress check is on the Horizon Calendar.

How this reconciles. The gov.uk search API returned 154 rows across six organisation queries for the four feeds, from 14 October onward. 19 of those were the same page returned under more than one query, leaving 135 distinct entries. 7 were chapter pages of two manuals (the homelessness code of guidance and the guidance on data protection in schools), each of which is listed once, so they came out, leaving 128. 2 were the same publications already in the 14 October log, so they came out, leaving 126. The mailroom digests carried 114 departmental feed items. 93 were dated inside the window, covering 91 distinct pages, and every one was already in the search result. Two of them are the publications already in the 14 October log, so 89 appear here, and 89 plus the 37 below makes 126. The other 21 were dated 12 and 13 October and arrived in the weekly feeds of 17 October, and every one of those is already in the 14 October log. 5 of the 126 rows are forthcoming-release announcements rather than things published in the window, which leaves 121 actual publications. The announcements are marked and kept, because they carry the dates the next figures land.
