What Landed

28 September · DWP

RedA £60m Fund for New Work and Health Support Opened Today, and Councils Can Bid

What changed?
DWP published the Pathways to Work Innovation Fund applicant bid pack today. The fund will invest up to £60 million over two years in grants to test new ways of helping disabled people and people with long-term health conditions move towards, enter, stay and progress in work. Local authorities and mayoral strategic authorities are named as eligible, alongside charities, businesses and consortia, and there is no minimum or maximum bid. The application window opened on 28 September 2026 and closes on 26 October 2026. A partnership directory is published by 5 October, shortlisting outcomes follow on 9 November, and an online portal opens in early October.
Why it matters?
Economic inactivity driven by long-term sickness is one of the structural weaknesses in Gateshead's labour market, and this is a four-week window for money aimed squarely at it. The fund wants ideas that improve outcomes beyond what would happen otherwise, and it asks for milestones and a budget, so a bid needs a clear proposal and a named delivery route rather than a statement of need. Four weeks is short for a council to design, partner and submit.
What should we do?
Put the question to employment and skills colleagues this week, and to NEMSA, whether anyone in the borough is bidding. If Gateshead bids or joins a consortium, the evidence case on inactivity and health is ours to supply. If nobody is bidding, that is worth recording as a decision rather than letting the window close unnoticed.
27 September · DWP · carried from 21 September

RedThe Jobs Guarantee Widens to Young People on Health Benefits From April 2027

What changed?
DWP announced that young disabled people and those with long-term health conditions will be offered a fast-tracked job under the Jobs Guarantee (27 September 2026). Eligible young people on health benefits will be offered a guaranteed job on a voluntary basis from week 13 after their Work Capability Assessment, with mentoring and support on mental health, finances and housing. The government covers 100% of wage costs up to 25 hours a week for six months. The expansion starts from April 2027. The release quotes the Young people and work interim report finding that 45% of NEET young people in the UK report a disability, up from 21.1% in 2013/14. The Jobs Guarantee page still says the national rollout goes live later in 2026, and the North East delivery partner is still not published.
Why it matters?
Last week's claimant count showed the whole of Gateshead's rise on the year sitting in the 18 to 24 band. The original scheme reaches only those on Universal Credit and looking for work for 18 months. This expansion reaches the young people who are out of the labour market on health grounds, which is a group the claimant count does not see. It gives the borough two cohorts to size, with two start dates.
What should we do?
Size both cohorts. The long-duration claimant group needs a Universal Credit duration cut on the work-search condition. The health group needs young people on health benefits after a Work Capability Assessment, which Stat-Xplore should support at local authority level. The delivery partner name is still a question for NEMSA or DWP district rather than a data pull.
24 September · ONS via NOMIS · carried from 21 September

RedThe 2026 Business Counts Are Live and Our Products Still Carry 2025

What changed?
Gateshead's count of VAT and/or PAYE registered enterprises for 2026 is 5,700 (NOMIS, UK Business Counts, enterprises, NM_142_1), against 5,580 in 2025. The North East count is 74,495 against 72,875, and England is 2,423,150 against 2,376,615. By size band Gateshead has 4,865 micro enterprises with 0 to 9 employees, 680 small, 120 medium-sized and 35 large. The count of local units, being individual sites, is 7,160 against 7,035 (NOMIS, UK Business Counts, local units, NM_141_1). The counts are rounded to the nearest five.
Why it matters?
This is a vintage change first and a finding second. The enterprise count grew by around 2% in Gateshead, in the North East and in England alike, so the borough's position has not moved in any way worth reporting. What has moved is the figure in our products. The 5,580 still appears in nine published products, including the NOMIS Quick Look, the business map and the scorecards, and the Quick Look's own vintage check now reports its business counts as stale.
What should we do?
Run the refresh. The Quick Look rebuild picks up the new vintage on its own. The static products need the figure changed and their vintage note updated, and the GVA 2024 dashboard, which pairs business counts with output, needs its size band table checked.

23 September · ONS

AmberGateshead's 2024 GVA Landed at the Bottom of Our Estimated Range

What changed?
Gateshead's balanced gross value added at current prices for 2024 is £5,221 million (ONS, Regional gross value added (balanced) by industry, local authorities by ITL1 region, published 23 September 2026). The latest year is provisional. The same release revises 2023 to £5,067 million, from £5,039 million in the previous release. In real terms the chained volume index for 2024 is 100.7, against 100 in 2023. The ONS has suppressed estimates for every area in eight ITL2 subregions, covering all of Wales and parts of South West England and Scotland, while it fixes an error converting to the new ITL25 geography, and expects to release a complete dataset within four to six weeks (ONS bulletin).
Why it matters?
Our internal estimates put 2024 at £5.2bn to £5.3bn, so the published figure sits at the bottom of that range. That is a fair result for the forecasting work and it should be reported as such. The suppression matters for comparison rather than for Gateshead's own figure. Seven of our twenty ONS economic statistical neighbours sit in the suppressed areas (the six Welsh authorities and Somerset), so any neighbour benchmark built now is missing over a third of the group. The NEMSA seven are unaffected.
What should we do?
Rebuild the neighbour comparisons in the GVA 2024 dashboard when the complete dataset lands, expected late October to early November, and hold any neighbour ranking until then. The subregional productivity release on 5 November follows closely and should be read alongside it.
23 September · DWP, DBT and DHSC

AmberNEMSA Is Named for a Shared Occupational Health Scheme for Small Firms

What changed?
The September update on the Keep Britain Working review sets out progress on a Workplace Health System, being built with eleven regional Vanguards, nine of them mayoral strategic authorities. It describes the Hull Resilience Hub, a buyer's pool that lets small firms buy occupational health services at the prices large firms pay, and says this is being looked at for replication in West Yorkshire and in the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority. A plan for phased roll-out is to be developed ahead of the Spending Review in 2027. The DWP release says around 300,000 people with a health condition leave work every year, and that WorkWell, backed by £259 million, will support up to 250,000 people. WorkWell rolls out across England from November 2026.
Why it matters?
Gateshead's business base is overwhelmingly micro and small firms, which are the firms least able to buy occupational health on their own. A shared scheme run through NEMSA is one of the few employer-side levers on sickness-related inactivity that reaches them. Being named for replication is not the same as being funded, so what is actually planned is the open question.
What should we do?
Ask NEMSA what the North East replication involves and when, and how it will sit alongside WorkWell from November. No data work is needed yet.
25 September · MHCLG

AmberA £210m High Streets Package, With the Application Routes Still to Come

What changed?
MHCLG announced a £210m package for high streets on 25 September 2026. It includes a new £125m Derelict Buildings Fund to support local authorities to transform derelict buildings, £65m to help communities rescue buildings and businesses at risk of closure such as pubs and sports clubs, and £20 million split between High Street Rental Auctions and a £10 million regional Co-operative Development Programme for mayors and strategic authorities. The money comes from MHCLG funding already earmarked for high streets. A full high streets strategy is promised later this year. No prospectus or application route has been published.
Why it matters?
The Derelict Buildings Fund is aimed at councils, and our July high streets deep dive covered the condition of Gateshead's centres. The co-operative programme runs through NEMSA. Until the prospectus appears we cannot tell whether the money is competitive, allocated or already committed.
What should we do?
Watch for the Derelict Buildings Fund prospectus and the high streets strategy, and be ready to offer vacancy evidence from the high streets work when a route opens.
26 September · MHCLG

AmberA New-Build Equity Loan Scheme Will Be Confirmed at the Budget

What changed?
MHCLG announced that Your First Home, an equity loan scheme for first-time buyers in England, will be confirmed at the Budget. It is expected to support 2.5% deposits backed by 20% government equity loans on new-build homes from participating developers, with an initial interest-free period. It will carry a household income cap and local property price caps, set out at the Budget with costs and timelines.
Why it matters?
The local price caps decide who the scheme reaches. A cap set against national or regional prices could sit well above typical Gateshead new-build prices, or a regional cap could exclude the borough's better-value stock. The release also presents the scheme as a stimulus to a new-build market facing rising construction costs, which touches local housing delivery.
What should we do?
Read the cap and income limit on Budget day, 28 October, and set them against current Gateshead new-build prices. This rides with the Budget read already on the calendar.
23 September · DfE

AmberVocational GCSEs Arrive in 2029, and Councils Help Broker Work Experience

What changed?
DfE announced new Vocational GCSEs to replace the current Technical Awards, available in some subjects from as early as September 2029 as part of technical pathways starting in September 2028. The first V Levels, in Education, Finance and Digital, start from September 2027. Alongside, a National Mission on Work Experience asks employers for an additional 200,000 placements so that all young people get workplace exposure between 14 and 16, with mayors and local authorities helping to link schools and employers.
Why it matters?
The qualification changes land too late for today's 18 to 24 cohort. The work experience mission is nearer term and gives councils a role, and Gateshead's 16 to 17 participation is one of the things our evidence says works, so it is a place where the borough has something to build on.
What should we do?
Ask NEMSA how placements will be brokered in the North East and whether councils are expected to hold employer lists. This follows on from last week's post-16 item.
26 September · DCMS

Amber1,000 Youth Workers From Spring 2027, and a £450m Fund Opening Soon

What changed?
DCMS announced training and employment for 1,000 new youth workers in England, with the first in post by spring 2027, working with young people aged 11 and over including those at highest risk of becoming NEET. A £450 million Youth Matters Fund to build and improve youth facilities in underserved areas "will open for applications soon". No date is given.
Why it matters?
This is prevention aimed at the NEET risk our youth work keeps returning to, and the fund targets underserved areas, a test Gateshead neighbourhoods may meet. With no opening date or criteria, there is nothing to prepare yet.
What should we do?
Watch for the fund opening and its eligibility test, and flag it to youth services colleagues when it lands.

Noted

27 September · Cabinet Office

GreenMeasures to Rewire the State Extend Buy-British Procurement to Utilities

What changed?
The Cabinet Office published Measures to rewire the state on 27 September 2026. Electricity, gas and water network companies will be expected to use their procurement to support jobs and skills in manufacturing, with legislation if they do not follow. The National Shipbuilding Office gets oversight of all future civil ship procurement, with a Shipbuilding and Maritime Technology Action Plan planned for later this year. A new Insourcing Unit in the Office for the Prime Minister and Cabinet will coordinate departmental insourcing, alongside the Public Interest Test on central government service contracts above £1 million.
Why it matters?
This extends the government's jobs-and-skills procurement stance from central government to the regulated utilities, which spend heavily in every region including ours. The insourcing measures apply to central government, not to councils.
What should we do?
Nothing now. Keep it with the social value and procurement material for any local supply chain work.

GreenUK-Only Builds for Two Maritime Projects

HM Treasury confirmed that three new floating docks for the submarine service at HM Naval Base Clyde will go to a UK-only competition, and that a new marine research vessel will be built in a British shipyard, operational in the early 2030s.
Why it matters?

What should we do?
Nothing.
27 September · MHCLG

GreenCouncils Can Act on Empty Homes After Six Months

What changed?
MHCLG announced reforms to Empty Dwelling Management Orders, which let councils take over management of long-term empty homes for up to seven years. The qualifying period falls from two years empty to six months, and evidence requirements on issues such as anti-social behaviour are removed. No commencement date is given.
Why it matters?
It is a council power rather than an economic measure, but it touches housing supply and the condition of neighbourhoods such as those in the Pride in Place area.
What should we do?
Nothing beyond flagging it to housing colleagues.
23 September · S&P Global

GreenPrivate Sector Growth Slowed in September as Costs Picked Up

What changed?
The S&P Global Flash UK PMI Composite Output Index for September 2026 is 51.7, down from 52.5 in August and a three-month low (S&P Global, 23 September 2026). The services business activity index is 51.7 and the manufacturing PMI is 52.0. Input price inflation rose for a second month to its highest since June, and total new work fell fractionally. Readings above 50 signal growth on the month. The flash is a UK figure with no regional breakdown.
Why it matters?
Slower growth with rising costs is the same pairing the Monetary Policy Committee flagged last week, and S&P say high costs are discouraging hiring. The North East read comes with the NatWest Regional Growth Tracker on 14 October.
What should we do?
Nothing now. Use the regional tracker in the next economic update.
