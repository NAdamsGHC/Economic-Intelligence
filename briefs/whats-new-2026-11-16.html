What's New, 16 November 2026

A short read of what’s landed, what’s coming, and what matters for Gateshead’s economy. This edition covers the eight days from 9 to 16 November. Every 18 to 24 year old who has been on Universal Credit and out of work for 18 months is to be guaranteed a funded six-month job from the end of November, with the Milburn Review of youth inactivity due this week. A joint regeneration framework for the Newcastle and Gateshead urban core is out for consultation until 6 January. The Cabinet Secretary put devolution by default at the heart of the future Civil Service. And short-term let guest nights in Gateshead rose by 11.6% in a year.

What Landed

14 November · DWP

AmberYoung People Out of Work for 18 Months on Universal Credit Are to Be Guaranteed a Job From the End of November

What changed?
DWP announced on 14 November 2026 that every eligible 18 to 24 year old in Great Britain claiming Universal Credit and looking for work will be guaranteed a fully funded six-month job from the end of November. Eligible means out of work and on Universal Credit for 18 months (DWP, 90,000 young people furthest from work to be given guaranteed job, no matter where they live). The scheme moves from its trial areas to all of Great Britain. It funds up to 25 hours of paid work a week for six months at the relevant minimum wage, and employers who hire a young person who has been out of work for six months can receive a £3,000 hiring payment. From April 2027 the qualifying period falls from 18 months out of work to 12, and young people receiving the Universal Credit health element can take part voluntarily. The government says the scheme will help more than 90,000 young people into work by March 2029. It is part of wider employment support for young people backed by £2.5bn, and more than 400 employers have backed it. The release says local authorities and mayors helped select the delivery partners. It also says the Milburn Review of rising youth inactivity reports next week.
Why it matters?
The guarantee is aimed at the 18 to 24 year olds furthest from work. The release names no North East delivery body or start date beyond the end of November, so the local route for young people and employers is not yet clear.
What should we do?
Employment support and business support colleagues will want to know, because employers can now offer placements. Nothing for us before the rollout.

Noted

6 November · NEMSA, late item

GreenThe Newcastle and Gateshead Regeneration Framework Is Out for Consultation Until 6 January

What changed?
NEMSA published a draft Newcastle and Gateshead Strategic Regeneration Framework for public consultation on 6 November 2026 (NEMSA, Have your say on the future of Newcastle and Gateshead). We missed it last week, so it is included here as a late item. The framework sets out a 25-year plan for the urban core of both places and was developed jointly by the Mayor, NEMSA, Newcastle City Council and Gateshead Council. It aims to raise the number of people living in the heart of the urban core to 16,000 by 2050 and to add more than 3 million square feet of commercial space. The Mayor names a new arena at Gateshead Quays. The consultation is an online survey and runs until 6 January 2027. One line of the page says 6 January 2026, which looks like a typo, since the other two say 2027.
Why it matters?
Gateshead Council is a joint author, and the framework covers the borough’s most important regeneration sites for the next 25 years. Its residential and commercial space targets will shape where jobs and homes go in the urban core.
What should we do?
Regeneration and economic development colleagues will know it already. Nothing for us before 6 January 2027.
10 November · Cabinet Office

GreenThe Cabinet Secretary Puts Devolution by Default at the Heart of the Future Civil Service

What changed?
The Cabinet Secretary gave the annual Cabinet Secretary’s Lecture at the London School of Economics on 10 November 2026 (Cabinet Office, Rewiring the State and the Future Civil Service). The speech says devolution by default is at the heart of the Civil Service’s work, and that 100 civil servants have been matched to roles in Mayoral Strategic Authorities, with more to follow. It describes a new Office for the Prime Minister and Cabinet that houses No.10, No.10 North and the Cabinet Office teams that directly serve the Prime Minister and the Cabinet,, and a Future Civil Service programme. A review of the permanent Civil Service, which began in July, reports to the Prime Minister before next summer, and a refreshed Civil Service Code is due in the spring. No Rewiring the State delivery plan was published in the window.
Why it matters?
Secondees into Mayoral Strategic Authorities would add capacity at bodies such as NEMSA, where more employment support and growth funding is due to sit, though the speech does not say how many go to each. The speech is a statement of intent and sets no dates for further devolution.
What should we do?
Nothing for us.
10 to 11 November · DfE

GreenColleges Asked to Bring 16 to 18 Year Olds Not in Education Back In, and New Post-16 Qualifications Out for Consultation

What changed?
The Education Secretary asked colleges on 11 November 2026 to bring 16 to 18 year olds who are not in education back in, to work with employers and to offer work experience that can lead to jobs (DfE, Colleges put at heart of drive to get young people learning). The release puts the number of 16 to 18 year olds not in education, employment or training at almost 150,000 and promises funding certainty for colleges that enrol them, without a figure. It says local leaders will get a greater role in shaping 16 to 19 provision. DfE also opened consultations on the content of new V Levels and Foundation Certificates in science and health and care, for teaching from September 2028, both closing on 23 December 2026, and on an EdTech and AI product safety certification scheme, closing on 4 February 2027.
Why it matters?
This sits alongside the job guarantee above and the Milburn Review. It covers the 16 to 18 group, where councils hold the duty to track young people’s participation.
What should we do?
Nothing for us. Skills colleagues may want the V Level consultations.
13 November · ONS

GreenShort-Term Let Guest Nights in Gateshead Rose by 11.6% in a Year

What changed?
ONS published Short-term lets through online collaborative economy platforms, UK: July 2023 to June 2026 on 13 November 2026, using data from Airbnb, Booking.com and Expedia Group. The North East had 2,888,820 guest nights in short-term lets from July 2025 to June 2026, up 17.3% on the year before, the largest rise of any English region. The UK figure was 104,617,530, up 10.2%. The bulletin goes to local authority level. Adding up the monthly figures in Table 1a of the accompanying dataset, Gateshead had 100,990 guest nights from July 2025 to June 2026, up from 90,480 the year before, a rise of 11.6% (our calculation from the published monthly figures, which ONS rounds).
Why it matters?
These are the stays a visitor levy would cover alongside hotels. Gateshead’s market is growing more slowly than the region’s but faster than the UK’s, and it gives a first base for what any levy on short-term lets might raise in the borough.
What should we do?
Nothing for us. Visitor economy colleagues may find the local figures useful ahead of any NEMSA draft levy plan.
12 November · ONS

GreenThe UK Economy Grew by 0.7% in the Third Quarter

What changed?
ONS estimated on 12 November 2026 that UK real GDP increased by 0.7% in July to September 2026 (GDP first quarterly estimate, UK: July to September 2026). Monthly GDP grew by 0.2% in September after no growth in August (GDP monthly estimate, UK: September 2026). In the three months to September, services output grew by 0.9% and production by 0.1%, while construction fell by 0.7%.
Why it matters?
Growth was led by services, while construction output fell by 0.7% over the quarter despite a 0.8% rise in September. There is no local figure, and Gateshead’s own output estimates lag by around two years.
What should we do?
Nothing for us.
10 November · MHCLG

GreenPlanning Application Fees Rise From 8 December

What changed?
MHCLG published Planning fees: national increases from 8 December 2026 on 10 November 2026. Fees for planning applications rise by varying amounts from 8 December, a flat fee of £310 is introduced for certain prior approval applications that were previously free, and all fees rise by a further 3.5% on 1 April 2027. For example, a full application for fewer than 10 homes will cost £752 for each home.
Why it matters?
The fees go to the council’s planning service and are paid by developers and businesses applying for permission. The increase is set nationally.
What should we do?
Nothing for us. Planning colleagues will have it.
13 November · BIST

GreenGuidance Updated for the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme

What changed?
The Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST) updated the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme business guidance on 13 November 2026, clarifying how the scheme works for firms supplied through a white label provider. The scheme reduces electricity costs for eligible manufacturers by exempting them from the Renewables Obligation, feed-in tariffs and the capacity market. Applications were due to open on 1 October 2026, and an October update tells businesses on the existing Supercharger scheme that they will not have confirmed exemption levels in January.
Why it matters?
Electricity costs are a live pressure on manufacturers, and the scheme is one of the few direct offers to them.
What should we do?
Business support colleagues may want to point eligible manufacturers to the guidance. Nothing for us.
9 to 13 November · MHCLG, HM Treasury, BIST and Cabinet Office

GreenLeaseholder Rights, Older People’s Housing and a Business Tax Consultation Closing on 21 November

What changed?
MHCLG announced on 11 November 2026 that more than three million leaseholders in England will get stronger rights to challenge unfair service charges from 1 April 2027, with further duties phased in to November 2028, when councils and housing associations must also provide standardised service charge demands, accounts and annual reports, all subject to Parliamentary approval (MHCLG, Flat owners handed massive boost to fight rip-off service charges), and published the government response to the Older People’s Housing Taskforce on 10 November. The Cabinet Office added a Public Interest Test template to PPN 024 on insourcing on 9 November. HM Treasury’s consultation on corporate tax simplification for business investment, published with the Budget, closes on 21 November.
Why it matters?
None changes the economic picture for Gateshead. The leaseholder duties will reach the council as a landlord from November 2028, which housing colleagues will want to know, and the tax consultation is aimed at firms and their advisers rather than councils.
What should we do?
Nothing for us.
