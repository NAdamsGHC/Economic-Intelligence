What's New · weekly round-up · covering 3 – 10 August

What's New — 10 August 2026

A short weekly read of what's coming, what's landed and what matters for Gateshead's economy. Each item says why it's here. Nothing local landed this week, but a date we were relying on has come off the calendar, and the machinery behind two of our core measures — the labour market survey and the productivity headline — is being changed in the open over the next three months.

The date that moved. Regional gross disposable household income, 1997 to 2024 no longer carries a confirmed day. Last week it was Wednesday 19 August; the ONS release page now reads "Provisional release date: August 2026" with no date attached and the release not yet published. That was the item the 20 August monthly brief was built around, so the brief either goes ahead without a new GDHI year or moves. Worth a check on the release page each morning from now on rather than assuming the 19th.

Landed this week

Circulated Tue 4 Aug

ONS Surveys and Economic Statistics Improvement Plan — third quarterly update

Why it matters: this is the plan that governs the two measures we caveat hardest. It confirms that ONS will publish in August an assessment of its readiness to move to the Transformed Labour Force Survey as the headline labour market measure in 2027 — that survey sits behind every Annual Population Survey estimate we publish with confidence intervals. It also names, among its forward objectives, moving to a new "component-based" approach as the method underpinning the headline productivity statistic, in line with international practice. The covering note circulated to North region contacts puts an explanatory article in September and migration in November, which would place it alongside the 5 November subregional productivity release. If the headline method changes in the same month our 2024 GVA-per-hour figure lands, the comparison we draw against 2023 needs stating carefully.

Also in it: 71% of regular tier 1 and tier 2 economic statistics are now accredited official statistics; all tier 1 and 2 economic statistics were published on time in the period; 17 lower-impact errors were corrected, around half the previous period's count. The Office for Statistics Regulation has said it will in future respond publicly on these updates only where intervention is needed.

Fri 7 Aug

Employment Rights Act implementation timeline updated

Why it matters: electronic and workplace balloting for statutory trade union ballots takes effect on 25 August 2026. It is a narrow change, but it is the first Employment Rights Act commencement inside our reporting window, and the timeline page is the one to watch for the measures with a real employer-cost dimension. The Act's phased commencements are a standing input to any business-facing read we produce.

Fri 7 Aug

DWP, Youth Jobs Grant guidance reissued

Why it matters: employer-facing guidance for the grant was reissued with corrections and realigned to the Find a Grant application form, and the employer FAQ moved from PDF to HTML. Relevant to the live NEET thread as delivery detail rather than as evidence. Separately, DWP has given a provisional August 2026 date for a report titled Young People and Work: Rapid Attitudinal Polling, which is worth reading when it appears — attitudinal evidence on this cohort is thin and we currently rely on administrative counts.

Fri 7 Aug

Public service productivity, quarterly, UK: January to March 2026

Why it matters: UK level only, so no Gateshead read, but it is the series most likely to be quoted at us in a conversation about council productivity. Knowing it has moved, and that it is a different construct from the regional productivity we publish, is the point of listing it.

Fri 7 Aug

Gross domestic expenditure on research and development, UK: 2024

Why it matters: mainly as something not to misread. UK R&D spend was £79.4 billion in 2024, 2.71% of GDP, with 70% performed by business. There is no regional or local authority breakdown in this bulletin, so it cannot be used for a North East or Gateshead innovation claim; the regional cut comes separately and later.

All tracked, none actioned.

A quiet week is still a week to use. No local statistics were published between 3 and 10 August, and the next Gateshead figure is the July claimant count on 18 August. The value in this edition is the two structural changes: the labour market survey behind our confidence-interval caveats is being assessed for transition, and the national productivity headline is being rebuilt on a new method in the same month our 2024 local productivity figure arrives. Both are worth understanding before they land rather than after. Next edition: Monday 17 August.
