What Landed

7 November · No.10

AmberThe Prime Minister Asks Councils for Plans to House Veterans Sleeping Rough Before Christmas

What changed?
No.10 announced on 7 November 2026 that the Prime Minister will write personally to councils in England on Armistice Day, 11 November, asking them to draw up specific plans to offer accommodation to any veterans sleeping rough ahead of the Christmas period (No.10, No one who has served our country should sleep rough on our streets). Councils are asked to use part of their rough sleeping funding, which the government puts at a record £4.4bn after an extra £874m. Separately, £8.7m goes to eight organisations across the UK through the Reducing Veteran Homelessness programme.
Why it matters?
This is a direct request to councils from the centre of government, and the accommodation is wanted ahead of the Christmas period. The release gives no date for the plans themselves. It sits outside the economic remit.
What should we do?
Housing colleagues will want to watch for the letter on 11 November. Nothing for us.

Noted

5 November · Bank of England

GreenBank Rate Rises to 4%

What changed?
The Bank of England’s Monetary Policy Committee voted by a majority of 8 to 1 at its meeting ending 4 November 2026 to raise Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points, to 4%. The other member voted to raise it to 4.25% (Monetary Policy Summary and minutes, November 2026). The Committee now expects CPI inflation to rise to around 5% by the middle of next year as higher energy prices feed through, then fall back. It says activity data have generally been stronger than expected and that policy is not on a pre-set path. The next decision is due on 17 December 2026.
Why it matters?
Higher borrowing costs reach firms with variable-rate loans or refinancing due, and households coming off fixed-rate mortgages. The minutes say many firms are absorbing higher energy costs through lower profit margins, and that some have not yet seen higher gas and electricity prices because of hedging or fixed-price contracts. That suggests the cost pressure on local firms may still be building.
What should we do?
Business support colleagues will want to know, alongside the government’s new guidance for firms hit by disruption from the Middle East conflict. Nothing further for us before 17 December.
By 9 November · ONS

GreenONS Cancels the 18 November BRES Release

What changed?
ONS has cancelled the release of Business Register and Employment Survey (BRES): provisional results 2025 and revised results 2024 for Great Britain, which was confirmed for 18 November 2026. The notice says the release is cancelled to allow time for further quality assurance, after routine checks identified further work needed before the provisional 2025 estimates can be finalised. ONS intends to publish at a later date and has not set one.
Why it matters?
BRES is the official source of employee jobs by industry for Gateshead, and the 2025 figures were the next update to the borough’s sector employment. Until ONS publishes, the 2024 estimates remain the latest.
What should we do?
Nothing until ONS sets a new date.
4 November · MHCLG

GreenThe Visitor Levy Bill Is Introduced, and the North East Mayor Backs a Levy

What changed?
The Visitor Levy Bill was introduced in the House of Commons on 4 November 2026 (MHCLG, New visitor levy powers to put money back into local communities). It lets mayors and local leaders charge a levy on overnight stays, set as a percentage of the cost of accommodation and not as a flat fee. The levy applies only to the first 28 consecutive days of a stay, and occasional hosts pay it only above an earnings threshold that areas can adjust. Student accommodation, care homes, children’s homes and school boarding houses are exempt nationally, and so are temporary accommodation, shelters and refuges. Before introducing a levy, an authority must publish a draft plan and an impact assessment, consult residents and businesses, and then publish an annual report on what it raises and spends. Ten mayors have committed that any levy in their area would be no higher than 5%. The Mayor of the North East is quoted in the announcement saying a levy would let the region compete with destinations that already raise money from visitors.
Why it matters?
The decision sits with NEMSA, and Gateshead’s hotels and other accommodation providers would account for and pay any levy. The Mayor’s statement makes a North East levy more likely. The draft plan and impact assessment are where the effect on Gateshead’s accommodation businesses would first be set out, and a rate set as a percentage of the room price raises less where room prices are lower.
What should we do?
Visitor economy and business support colleagues will want to know the rules ahead of any NEMSA draft plan. The ONS release on short-term lets through online platforms on 13 November gives a first measure of that part of the market.
5 November · No.10

GreenEmployers Will Have to Publish Salaries in Job Adverts

What changed?
No.10 announced on 5 November 2026 plans to make publishing salaries in job adverts a legal requirement (No.10, New ‘say what you pay’ law to help young people into work). It cites CIPD research (Pay, performance and transparency, 2024) finding that 41% of employers always publish salary ranges and 18% always publish specific salaries, and says early analysis shows 94% of respondents to the government consultation backed the requirement, including 93% of businesses. The Prime Minister also asked more businesses to back the government’s target of 200,000 work experience opportunities. No start date for the law is given.
Why it matters?
The duty would apply to every Gateshead employer that advertises jobs. The government links it to young people applying for their first job and to pay gaps that disadvantage women. Nothing changes for firms until a start date is set.
What should we do?
Flag to business support colleagues so they can tell employers it is coming. Nothing for us.

5 November · BIST

GreenThe North East Has the Lowest Business Density of the English Regions

What changed?
BIST published Business population estimates 2026 on 5 November 2026, as official statistics in development. The North East’s number of private sector businesses at the start of 2026 is 162,890 (Business population estimates 2026, Table 9), the fewest of the English regions. The North East’s business density at the start of 2026 is 714 private sector businesses per 10,000 resident adults (Table 8), the lowest of the English regions, against 986 for the UK. The UK total fell by 1.8% on 2025, which is 104,000 businesses (statistical release).
Why it matters?
The estimates go no lower than region, so there is no Gateshead figure. They include unregistered businesses, which are estimated from survey data, and the release puts the North East’s coefficient of variation at 6%, the widest of any region (a measure of how uncertain the estimate is). Gateshead’s own business count comes from UK Business Counts, which covers VAT or PAYE registered businesses only and is published separately.
What should we do?
Nothing for us.
18 September · ONS

GreenGateshead Mortgage Buyers Borrow a Higher Share of the Price Than Buyers in England

What changed?
ONS published Mortgage statistics, by local authority, UK on 18 September 2026, as official statistics in development using Financial Conduct Authority data. We missed it at the time, so it is included here as a late item. Gateshead’s number of mortgage sales for 2025 is 2,248 (ONS mortgage statistics 2025, Table 1a), up from 1,970 in 2024, and 1,207 of those were first-time buyers (Table 1b). Gateshead’s median purchase price for mortgage sales in 2025 is £170,000 (Table 3b), unchanged on 2024 and against £300,000 for England. Gateshead’s mortgage loan-to-value ratio for 2025 is 84.92% (Table 2a), against 80% for England. Gateshead’s property value to income ratio for 2025 is 3.56 (Table 2c), against 4.59 for England.
Why it matters?
Homes bought with a mortgage in Gateshead are cheaper relative to buyers’ incomes than in England, but buyers borrow a larger share of the price. That suggests more Gateshead buyers sit on higher loan-to-value mortgages, which are usually priced higher and are more exposed when rates rise, as they did on 5 November.
What should we do?
Nothing for us. Housing colleagues may find the series useful.
4 November · ONS

GreenONS Says Labour Force Survey Response Is Back to Pre-Covid Levels

What changed?
ONS published the 2026 Deane-Stone Lecture, given at the National Institute of Economic and Social Research on 4 November 2026. It says achieved response rates for the Labour Force Survey are back at the pre-Covid level and up over 80% since the low point in 2023, and that sampling variability is down by over a quarter. The move to the Transformed Labour Force Survey is planned for 2027, with return rates in later waves approaching 40%. The lecture also says ONS is working to produce more timely regional GDP figures, and is talking to government about its priorities for local statistics under Rewiring the State.
Why it matters?
Gateshead’s employment and inactivity rates come from the Annual Population Survey, which draws on the Labour Force Survey, so better response should in time help narrow the wide margins on our local estimates. More timely regional GDP would shorten the long lag on Gateshead’s output figures, but the lecture gives no dates.
What should we do?
Nothing for us.
3 to 5 November · MHCLG and DWP

GreenNew Consultations on Surplus Compulsory Purchase Land and on Pensions

What changed?
MHCLG opened a consultation on reform of the Crichel Down Rules on 4 November 2026, closing on 6 January 2027. The rules give former owners of land bought by, or under the threat of, compulsory purchase the chance to buy back land the authority no longer needs, and the consultation proposes to simplify and speed up that process. DWP opened consultations on guided retirement (closing 22 December 2026), on the Retirement Collective Money Purchase Schemes regulations (closing 15 December 2026) and on pension trustees’ investment and fiduciary duties (closing 28 January 2027).
Why it matters?
The Crichel Down Rules bear on how councils assemble land for regeneration and what happens to land left over afterwards. The pension consultations have no local read.
What should we do?
Flag the Crichel Down consultation to regeneration and legal colleagues. Nothing for us.
