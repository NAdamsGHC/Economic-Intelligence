23 October · DWP · carried from 28 September

RedRound One of the Innovation Fund Closes Today, With a £50 Million Second Round Expected Early in 2027

What changed?
DWP added a link to the online application portal to the applicant bid pack and the fund page on 23 October 2026. Applications can still go by email or post. The fund guidance says applications are being accepted until 26 October 2026, which is today. That is the first funding round, of up to £10 million. The guidance says up to a further £50 million will be available in a second round, expected to open to bids early in 2027. The partnership directory has not been updated since 16 October, so it still names no partner from the NEMSA area.
Why it matters?
The fund puts up to £60 million over two years into new ways of helping disabled people and people with long-term health conditions into work. Economic inactivity driven by long-term sickness is one of the structural weaknesses in Gateshead’s labour market. The item has carried for four weeks and no decision has been recorded here.
What should we do?
Whichever way it went, record whether Gateshead bid, joined a bid or passed in round one. The second round is the larger one, so the evidence case on inactivity and health is worth having ready before it opens.

20 October · ONS via NOMIS

AmberClaimants Up to 5,050 in September, and Inactivity Down on the Annual Population Survey

What changed?
Gateshead’s claimant count for Sep-26 is 5,050, which is 4.0% of residents aged 16-64 (claimant count, ONS, 20 October 2026). That compares with 4,990 in Aug-26 and 4,935 in Sep-25. The North East is at 3.7% and England at 4.1%. The same release moved the Annual Population Survey on to Jul-25 to Jun-26. Gateshead’s 16-64 economic inactivity rate for Jul-25 to Jun-26 is 18.3% (±3.7pp), down from 24.0% (±4.1pp) a year earlier, against the North East at 24.3% (±1.1pp) and England at 20.5% (±0.3pp) (Annual Population Survey). The 16-64 employment rate is 76.9% (±4.1pp), up from 71.4% (±4.4pp), and the 16+ unemployment rate is 5.8% (±2.4pp), against 6.2% (±2.6pp) a year earlier. The claimant count is an administrative count with no sampling interval.
Why it matters?
The fall in inactivity is just large enough to be statistically significant, and it puts Gateshead significantly below the North East. We cannot say whether Gateshead’s inactivity is above or below England’s. The rise in employment is not statistically established. One survey year at borough level moves a lot, and since 11 August the survey is published as official statistics that are no longer accredited, so the inactivity fall needs another period before it carries a headline. The claimant count is up by around 115 on a year, while the rate is unchanged at 4.0% on the month.
What should we do?
Use both in the Quarterly Economic Update on 29 October, with the intervals, and describe the employment rise as not statistically established.
22 October · ONS

AmberProvisional 2026 Pay for Gateshead Residents Is £735.60 a Week, Still Below England

What changed?
Gateshead’s median gross weekly pay for full-time employees living in the borough for 2026 is £735.60, and for full-time jobs based in the borough it is £737.40 (ASHE table 8 and table 7, 2026 provisional, ONS, 22 October 2026). We can be 95% confident that the resident figure lies between approximately £681 and £790, and the workplace figure between approximately £675 and £800 (our calculation from the published coefficients of variation). England’s resident figure is £815.50 and the North East’s is £732.50, with intervals of approximately ±£5 and ±£16. ONS puts the change on a year earlier at 10.2% for Gateshead residents and 8.2% for Gateshead jobs, against 5.7% for England residents. NOMIS had not loaded the 2026 figures when this edition was written.
Why it matters?
Resident pay is still statistically below England, because the top of Gateshead’s interval sits below England’s figure. We cannot tell Gateshead apart from the North East. A one-year rise of 10% at borough level should be treated with caution, because ONS publishes no quality measure for the change and the borough estimate has a wide interval. These figures are provisional and will be revised next October.
What should we do?
Use the 2026 provisional figures in the quarterly update and the December LEA, and pull them through NOMIS once loaded so the query can be reproduced.
22 October · No.10, HM Treasury

AmberPlace-Based Green Book Reforms Promised for the Budget

What changed?
No.10 and HM Treasury announced on 22 October 2026 that a package of Green Book reforms will come at the Budget. The guidance will be updated so that places, including local and strategic authorities, can build investment plans around a fuller picture of their local economy and make place-based business cases, rather than having each project considered on its own. No10 North will help places make use of the changes, including places without a mayor. The announcement says this builds on cutting the headline discount rate from 3.5% to 3%, which the Chancellor announced in his growth speech of 7 September 2026. The published Green Book was last updated on 5 February 2026, so neither change is in the guidance yet.
Why it matters?
Every business case Gateshead or NEMSA puts to government is appraised against the Green Book. A lower discount rate gives more weight to benefits that arrive years after the money is spent. The announcement names low employment and skills shortages as the kind of linked problems a place-based case would treat together, which describes parts of Gateshead’s economy. How much difference it makes depends on guidance that has not been published.
What should we do?
Read the revised guidance in full when it is published with the Budget, and update our appraisal templates to it. A note on what the changes mean for live and planned Gateshead business cases is a proposal for commissioning.
