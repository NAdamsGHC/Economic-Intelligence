And the North East has the highest share of children in long-term workless households of any UK region.

What Landed

1 October · BIST

RedManufacturers Can Now Register for Cheaper Electricity, and Registrations Close on 30 November

What changed?
The Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST) opened applications for the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme on 1 October 2026. Eligible manufacturers in the Industrial Strategy's eight growth sectors, and in the foundational industries that supply them, will be exempt from the indirect costs of the Renewables Obligation and Feed-in Tariffs from April 2027, and of the Capacity Market from October 2027 (guidance for applicants). BIST puts the saving at up to 25% of their electricity bills. Registrations close on 30 November. Firms confirmed as eligible also get a lump sum equal to around a year's relief, and only firms applying this year can get it. Eligibility starts from a final list of 4-digit industry and product codes, which will not change for 2027. The industry must also clear an electricity-intensity threshold, and each site must have used more than 16.5 MWh of grid electricity over six months. BIST's impact assessment estimates that small and micro businesses make up 98% of businesses in the frontier sectors and 99% in the foundational ones.
Why it matters?
The scheme is targeted by sector, and nearly all firms in the eligible sectors are small businesses, which are the firms least likely to hear about a registration window on their own. A firm that misses 30 November loses the extra year's lump sum for good. Small manufacturers are also the ones S&P Global found contracting sharply across the UK in September.
What should we do?
Pass the scheme to business support colleagues now, so the message reaches Gateshead manufacturers well before 30 November. We could also match the eligible industry codes against UK Business Counts to give business support a count of Gateshead firms by eligible sector. That count would be an upper bound, because the intensity and site tests cannot be applied to it. That is a proposal for commissioning.

28 September · HM Treasury

AmberMayors Get Apprenticeship Brokerage Funding From Spring 2027, NEMSA Included

What changed?
The Chancellor announced a package to back young people into work on 28 September 2026. Apprenticeship brokerage, piloted in some mayoral strategic authorities after Budget 2025, is extended to all 14 existing mayoral strategic authorities with directly elected mayors from spring 2027, backed by £100 million over two years. The National Wealth Fund will use its current capitalisation to create and support a further 130,000 jobs by 2030. The Union Learning Fund is restored in England with £15 million a year. The package also names a £300 million Rolls-Royce investment in Derby, Bristol, Inchinnan and Rotherham, none of it in the North East.
Why it matters?
Youth employment is one of the live threads in our work, and brokerage puts NEMSA in charge of matching young people with small employers across the region. Whether Gateshead's young people are reached depends on how the service is run and where it looks for employers.
What should we do?
Ask NEMSA how the service will run and whether placements will be reported by local authority, so we can track it in the youth employment work.
1 October · ONS

AmberThe North East Has the Highest Share of Children in Long-Term Workless Households

What changed?
The North East's percentage of children living in long-term workless households for 2025 is 17.0% (ONS, Children living in long-term workless households in the UK: 2025), the highest of any UK region. The UK figure is 10.1% and the lowest is the South East at 6.1%. That is 84,117 children in the North East, against 75,157 and 16.6% in 2024 (ONS, Table D). A long-term workless household is one with at least one person aged 16 to 64, where everyone aged 16 and over has been workless for more than a year or has never worked.
Why it matters?
The figures come from the household Annual Population Survey, the ONS publishes no confidence intervals for them, and the regional series moves a lot from year to year (the North East was 12.0% in 2023). We cannot say whether the region rose on 2024. The level is the finding, because the North East has been well above every other region in both years. It fits the household worklessness our youth and inactivity work keeps returning to. This is a regional figure, and the borough-level picture comes from the local authority workless households tables.
What should we do?
Use it as regional context in the Quarterly Economic Update on 29 October, next to the local authority workless households figures.
29 September · No.10

AmberA National Care Service in the Next Parliament, Paid for by Changing the Triple Lock From 2030

What changed?
The Prime Minister set out plans for a new National Care Service on 29 September 2026. It will be established in the next Parliament and introduced in phases, providing older people with free personal care based on need rather than ability to pay. The Triple Lock stays until April 2030. After that the State Pension rises by at least inflation or 2.5% each year, with a new link to keep pace with earnings, and the government will legislate for the change in this Parliament. It estimates the change cuts State Pension spending by £15 billion a year by the end of the 2030s, and the savings fund the care service. Baroness Casey's independent Commission will recommend how and when the service is built up, and reports in summer 2027.
Why it matters?
Adult social care is a council function, and charges for personal care are part of how it is paid for today, so the design matters to the council's finances. Nothing changes before the Commission reports.
What should we do?
Nothing now. Watch for the Commission's next phase and its report in summer 2027, which is on the Horizon Calendar.

1 October · DWP

GreenSmall Firms Get £2,000 for Each Young Apprentice They Take On

What changed?
DWP announced a £2,000 hiring payment on 1 October 2026 for non-levy paying employers, typically small and medium-sized businesses, for each new apprentice aged 16 to 24 at the start of training. The apprentice must have joined the employer no more than 90 days before training began. Employers do not apply. The training provider passes the money on in two £1,000 instalments, at 90 days and at one year (or 242 days for shorter and foundation apprenticeships), provided the apprentice is still employed. The release gives no start date, and the Federation of Small Businesses, quoted in it, describes the payment as having come into effect. A refreshed apprenticeships.gov.uk launched alongside it.
Why it matters?
The Federation of Small Businesses says it stacks with other support worth up to £6,000 per apprentice. Together with the brokerage funding, it bears directly on youth apprenticeships in small firms.
What should we do?
Add it to the message to business support alongside the electricity cost scheme.
30 September and 1 October · S&P Global, Lloyds Bank

GreenManufacturing Kept Growing in September, but Small Manufacturers Contracted Sharply

What changed?
The S&P Global UK Manufacturing PMI for September 2026 is 51.9, up from 51.7 in August, and has signalled expansion for 11 months (S&P Global, 1 October 2026). Readings above 50 signal growth on the month. Growth was centred on investment goods, and small manufacturers saw production and new business contract sharply. Separately, the Lloyds Bank Business Barometer put overall UK business confidence at a net balance of 41% in September, down 12 points and its lowest since April 2025, with higher energy costs and uncertainty behind the fall. Sentiment improved in the North East and the West Midlands (Lloyds Bank Market Insights newsletter, 30 September 2026). Both are UK surveys, and regional readings rest on far smaller samples than the national figures.
Why it matters?
Small manufacturers under cost pressure make up most of the eligible sectors for the electricity cost scheme. The North East read from the NatWest Regional Growth Tracker lands on 14 October.
What should we do?
Nothing now.
30 September and 2 October · ONS

GreenUK Growth Revised Up to 0.5% in the Second Quarter

What changed?
UK gross domestic product is estimated to have grown by 0.5% in Quarter 2 (Apr to June) 2026, revised up by 0.1 percentage points, following unrevised growth of 0.6% in Quarter 1 (ONS, GDP quarterly national accounts, UK: April to June 2026, 30 September 2026). Growth for 2025 as a whole is revised down by 0.1 percentage points to 1.2%. Real household disposable income per head increased by 1.0% in the quarter, after a fall of 0.8% in Quarter 1. On 2 October the ONS put median household disposable income in the UK for the financial year ending 2025 at £39,200, an increase of 3.5% after adjusting for inflation, on an equivalised basis that adjusts for household size (ONS, Average household income, UK). The ONS warns that the survey sample for that year fell after the Survey of Living Conditions stopped, so the finer breakdowns carry more uncertainty.
Why it matters?
National context only. The Blue Book, provisionally due on 30 October, sets the national accounts that the next regional accounts are built on.
What should we do?
Nothing. Use as national context in the quarterly update.

How this reconciles. The gov.uk search API returned 210 rows across six organisation queries for the four feeds. 22 of those were the same page returned under more than one query, leaving 188 distinct entries. 7 were chapter sub-pages of the homelessness code of guidance, which is listed once, so they came out, leaving 181. The announcements are marked and kept, because they carry the dates the next figures land.

Why both sources. Neither source is complete alone. The business department's content now publishes under the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST) organisation as well as the old DBT one, and eight items in the window carry only the BIST tag, the opening of the electricity cost scheme among them. The search now queries both slugs. The weekly MHCLG and DfE feeds deliver on a Saturday, so most of their items in this window have not reached the mailroom yet and are in the log from the search API alone. Both MHCLG organisation slugs are queried, because the live one has no "and" in it and the wrong form returns nothing at all without raising an error.
