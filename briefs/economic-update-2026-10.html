The quarterly update, built on the October labour market release and the new Annual Population Survey quarter. It covers everything since the September monthly edition of 21 September. The Labour Market

GreenEconomic Inactivity Has Fallen, and the Fall Is Statistically Significant

What changed?
Gateshead’s economic inactivity rate for people aged 16-64 for Jul-25 to Jun-26 is 18.3% (±3.7pp), down from 24.0% (±4.1pp) a year earlier (ONS, Annual Population Survey, via NOMIS). The North East is 24.3% (±1.1pp) and England 20.5% (±0.3pp). The share of inactive people who are long-term sick is 32.6% (±10.7pp), against 36.3% (±2.4pp) for the North East and 28.2% (±0.7pp) for England.
Why it matters?
The fall of 5.7 percentage points is just larger than the combined interval of the two years, so it is statistically significant, and Gateshead is now significantly below the North East. We cannot say whether it is above or below England. The change on the quarter is not statistically established, and the long-term sick share has not moved by anything we can detect. One year of a small survey can swing like this, and since August the survey is published as official statistics that are no longer accredited, so the fall needs to hold for another period before it is treated as settled.
What should we do?
Employability colleagues can treat this as an encouraging sign, but not yet as evidence that the position has changed. The next survey quarter lands on 19 January 2027.

GreenThe Claimant Count Is Level With England and Has Barely Moved

What changed?
Gateshead’s claimant count for Sep-26 is 5,050, and claimants as a proportion of residents aged 16-64 is 4.0% (ONS, claimant count, via NOMIS, not seasonally adjusted). That compares with 4,990 in Jun-26 and 4,935 in Sep-25. The North East is at 3.7% and England at 4.1%. The August figure has been revised down from the 5,100 published in September to 4,990.
Why it matters?
The rate is unchanged on the quarter and up 0.1 percentage points on the year. Gateshead stays above the North East and close to England, as it has been. The revision to August means the sharp July to August rise reported in the September edition was smaller than first published.
What should we do?
Nothing new for us this quarter.

GreenNo Detectable Change in Employment, and Payrolled Jobs Down Slightly

What changed?
Gateshead’s employment rate for people aged 16-64 for Jul-25 to Jun-26 is 76.9% (±4.1pp), up from 71.4% (±4.4pp) a year earlier, against the North East at 71.8% (±1.1pp) and England at 75.8% (±0.3pp). The unemployment rate for people aged 16 and over is 5.8% (±2.4pp), against 6.2% (±2.6pp) a year earlier. Gateshead’s count of payrolled employees for Aug-26 is 86,302, down from 86,448 in May-26 and 86,707 in Aug-25 (ONS, PAYE Real Time Information, seasonally adjusted). The North East fell from 1,126,245 to 1,120,076 over the same year.
Why it matters?
The rise in the employment rate is inside the combined interval, so it is not statistically established, and unemployment has not changed by anything we can detect. Gateshead’s employment rate is significantly above the North East’s and cannot be told apart from England’s. The payroll figures have no sampling error, and they show approximately 0.5% fewer payrolled jobs than a year ago, the same rate of fall as the region and the UK, which fell approximately 0.5% over the same year in the same tables (ONS, PAYE Real Time Information, September 2026 tables). The local payroll series next updates on 15 December.
What should we do?
Nothing new for us this quarter.

Standing Local Indicators

GreenHouse Prices Are Rising Faster Than England’s

What changed?
Gateshead’s average house price for Aug-26 is £156,036, up 4.4% on the year and down 0.2% on the month (HM Land Registry, UK House Price Index). The North East is £165,640, up 3.2%, and England £293,525, up 0.4%. Gateshead had 60 permanent dwellings started and 80 completed in Apr-26 to Jun-26, against 60 and 100 a year earlier (MHCLG, live table 253a).
Why it matters?
Prices here are rising much faster than England’s from a level a little over half of it, so the gap is narrowing. Starts are the same as a year earlier. Housing was again the weakest part of construction nationally in September, with the house building index at 40.7, where 50 means no change (S&P Global UK Construction PMI, 6 October 2026). Quarterly starts in Gateshead are small numbers and move a lot, so one quarter says little on its own.
What should we do?
Housing and planning colleagues will want to know that prices are rising faster here than nationally.

GreenPension-Age Claimants Are Not Updated This Quarter

What changed?
Nothing. The NOMIS state pension claimant series stopped updating in November 2018, and there is still no current source we can pull without a licence key.
Why it matters?
Rather than quote an old number with a current label, the row stays blank until a free source is in place.
What should we do?
None.

Cost of Living

GreenInflation Has Climbed to 3.6%, and Pay Is Only Just Ahead

What changed?
The UK’s CPIH annual rate for Sep-26 is 3.6%, up from 3.3% in Aug-26, and CPI is 3.5% (ONS, consumer price inflation, 21 October 2026). Motor fuels made the largest upward contribution. Regular pay across the economy grew 3.7% in the year to Jun-26 to Aug-26, which is 0.6% in real terms after CPIH (ONS, labour market overview, 20 October 2026). Gateshead’s provisional median gross weekly pay for full-time employees living in the borough for 2026 is £735.60 (ONS, ASHE 2026 provisional, table 8), and we can be 95% confident it lies between approximately £681 and £790. The North East is £732.50 and England £815.50, so Gateshead is below England and cannot be told apart from the region.
Why it matters?
With prices rising faster again and pay growth flat, the real gain in pay is small. The Budget’s £100 Household Energy Payment for homes in council tax bands A to D starts in January, and approximately 96% of Gateshead’s homes are in those bands (VOA, Council Tax stock of properties, at 9 September 2025). MHCLG’s council tax information letter 6/2026 says councils are expected to start paying from January 2027.
What should we do?
Nothing new for us this quarter.

Business Conditions and National Context

RedNorth East Business Activity Is Growing Again, While Manufacturers Face Rising Costs

What changed?
The North East’s business activity index for Sep-26 is 52.0, a renewed increase in output (NatWest UK Regional Growth Tracker, 14 October 2026). Nationally, the S&P Global UK Manufacturing PMI for Oct-26 is 52.8, a five-month high, up from 51.9. The UK Services PMI business activity index for Sep-26 is 52.1, down from 52.5. The UK Construction PMI for Sep-26 is 46.1, up from 44.3, with house building at 40.7 and commercial building at 48.5, and civil engineering also falling more slowly. These are business surveys where 50 means no change on the month. UK GDP grew 0.7% in the three months to Aug-26 (ONS, GDP monthly estimate). UK vacancies fell 21,000 to 691,000 in Jul-26 to Sep-26. Producer input prices rose 8.3% and factory gate prices 4.6% in the year to Sep-26 (ONS, producer price inflation).
Why it matters?
The regional tracker is the only one of these surveys with a North East figure, and it points to renewed growth in the region. The national surveys describe rising output with rising costs. Manufacturers’ input costs are growing almost twice as fast as the prices they charge, which squeezes margins, and hiring is flat in manufacturing and still falling in services and construction. The Manufacturing PMI survey closed before the Budget. The latest Business Insights and Conditions Survey bulletin could not be reached when this edition was built, so it is not quoted.
What should we do?
Business support colleagues can point eligible manufacturers to the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme before applications close on 30 November.

Reading the figures. The Annual Population Survey figures are estimates from a sample, so each carries a 95% confidence interval, shown as ± percentage points. The survey periods are rolling 12-month windows, so a change on the quarter compares two windows that share nine months of the same sample, which is why the series looks steady and then jumps. The claimant count and payrolled employees are administrative counts with no sampling interval, though both are revised after first publication. The claimant rate is claimants as a proportion of residents aged 16-64, and the national comparator is England. The full series are on the Quarterly Economic Update dashboard.
