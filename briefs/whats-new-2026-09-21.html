What Landed

Confirmed for Wednesday 23 September · ONS

RedThe First Official 2024 GVA Figure for Gateshead Lands on Wednesday

What changed?
The ONS release calendar now confirms Regional economic activity by gross domestic product, UK: 1998 to 2024 for 09:30 on Wednesday 23 September 2026. This is the delayed balanced GVA release. It carries the first official 2024 local authority figure for Gateshead, and it revises the back series at the same time. Gateshead's balanced gross value added at current basic prices for 2023 is £5.0bn (ONS regional accounts), which has been the latest available all year.
Why it matters?
This is the number the whole autumn cycle has been waiting on, and it settles an open question of our own. We have been carrying internal estimates of £5.2bn to £5.3bn for 2024, derived from our regression and forecast work. Wednesday tells us whether those estimates were any good. The revised back series matters as much as the new year, because a revision moves the base that every growth rate and every gap-to-peers comparison in our published work is calculated from. Anything quoting a 2023 or earlier GVA figure becomes a stale vintage on Wednesday morning.
What should we do?
Read it on release and give the Gateshead read the same day. Three things to report, being the 2024 level, what the revision does to the back series we have been quoting, and how our internal estimates performed against the published figure. That last one is the honest test of the forecasting work and should be reported whichever way it falls.
15 September · ONS via NOMIS

RedThe August Claimant Count Rise Is Entirely a Youth Rise

What changed?
Gateshead's claimant count for August 2026 is 5,100 people, and claimants as a proportion of residents aged 16-64 is 4.1% (NOMIS, claimant count by sex and age, NM_162_1, not seasonally adjusted). That is up from 4,890 in July 2026 and 5,000 in August 2025. The comparators for August 2026 are the North East at 63,800 and 3.7%, and the United Kingdom at 1,698,450 and 3.9%. The age breakdown is where the story is. Gateshead's claimant count for those aged 18-24 is 1,040 in August 2026, against 960 in July 2026 and 940 in August 2025.
Why it matters?
The borough total rose by 100 on the year. The 18-24 band rose by 100 on the year on its own, so every other age band together is flat or falling. That is the first hard local number to land on the 18-24 exposure we have been describing since July, and it is an administrative count rather than a survey estimate, so there is no confidence interval to hide behind. Gateshead also sits above both the regional and the national rate. The youth series is small enough that one month proves nothing, but the direction has now held for three.
What should we do?
The monthly economic update published today leads on this and carries the seasonality test that was outstanding from last week. The next step is a ward-level cut, which NM_162_1 supports, to establish whether the youth rise is borough-wide or concentrated. That would turn a headline into something a service could act on.
16 September · DWP

RedThe Jobs Guarantee Goes National in November, Aimed at 18 to 24 Year Olds

What changed?
DWP updated the Jobs Guarantee publication page on 16 September 2026, replacing the national rollout grant instructions to clarify the moderation process. The rollout runs from November 2026 across 25 areas. It offers a fully funded six-month paid job at 25 hours a week at the National Minimum Wage, with employer National Insurance and pension covered, to 18 to 24 year olds who have been on Universal Credit for 18 months or more. The first phase, from 26 August 2026, covered six areas and none of them was in the North East.
Why it matters?
This is a national intervention pointed at exactly the cohort where Gateshead's own data says we are exposed. The claimant count item above says the 18-24 band accounts for the whole of the borough's rise on the year. A fully funded six-month job for long-duration claimants in that band is the largest single lever available to us on that problem, and it arrives in about six weeks. Two facts we do not hold are the lead delivery partner for the North East area, which employers have to approach directly, and the size of the eligible Gateshead cohort, which needs a Universal Credit duration cut rather than the headline claimant count.
What should we do?
Establish both before November. The delivery partner name is a question for NEMSA or DWP district rather than a data pull, because the grant competition closed in July and the partner list is not published on gov.uk. The cohort size is ours to derive, and it is the number that tells colleagues whether this is worth building employer engagement around.

What changed?
MHCLG updated its Capacity to Build guidance today to open the 2026-27 Council Housebuilding Support Fund bidding round. Applications are open from 21 September to 2 November 2026. It is revenue funding for early-stage housing development work such as feasibility, site investigation and pipeline preparation, intended to strengthen later Social and Affordable Homes Programme bids. Flag it to housing and development colleagues this week and ask whether Gateshead intends to bid. If yes, offer the site and pipeline evidence. If nobody is bidding, that is worth knowing too, and it is a finding rather than a gap.

17 September · ONS

AmberThe New Productivity Method Halves the Measured Post-2008 Slowdown

What changed?
The ONS published Component approach to measuring labour productivity, UK: 1997 to 2024 on 17 September 2026, as research rather than official statistics. It rebuilds labour input from Workforce Jobs benchmarked to BRES rather than from the Labour Force Survey total, and it builds hours from Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings usual hours with leave, bank holidays, sickness and overtime deducted separately instead of taking reported actual hours. By 2024 the jobs estimate is around 4.7% higher than the Labour Force Survey total it replaces. On output per hour, growth from 2009 to 2019 becomes 1.3% a year against 0.7% on the current approach, while growth from 1997 to 2007 becomes 2.0% against 2.1%. Over 1997 to 2024 cumulative growth becomes 40.7% against 34.0%.
Why it matters?
This roughly halves the size of the post-financial-crisis productivity slowdown as measured, without making it disappear. Productivity is the spine of our assessment work and of the case for intervention, so a measurement change of that size changes the national story any local analysis is set against. The timetable is the part that matters for us. The whole economy figures switch to the new method in November 2026, and industry figures follow at the February 2027 quarterly release. The paper itself covers neither industry nor subnational estimates, and the accompanying ONS blog says improved subnational data follows industry during 2027.
What should we do?
Nothing is required now, which is why this drops from red. Two dates go on the forward plan. November 2026, when the whole economy series changes under us. And February 2027, when industry estimates change, which is the first point the new method could touch anything we publish. The subregional productivity release on 5 November 2026 still comes on the current method, so it is comparable with what we hold.
16 and 17 September · ONS and Bank of England

AmberThree MPC Members Voted to Raise Rates, With Inflation Back Above 3%

What changed?
The UK CPI 12-month inflation rate for August 2026 is 3.1%, up from 2.9% in July 2026 (ONS consumer price inflation, published 16 September 2026). CPIH is 3.3%, core CPI is 2.6% and CPI services is 3.4%, with the last two unchanged on the month. Transport, and motor fuels within it, gave the largest upward contribution. The following day the Monetary Policy Committee voted by six to three to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%, with the three dissenting members voting to raise it to 4% (Bank of England, September 2026). The Committee said inflation is likely to rise further and could reach slightly above 4% in 2027 Q1, and that risks are tilted to the upside. It also agreed unanimously to reduce its gilt holdings to zero over a multi-year path.
Why it matters?
The direction of the dissent is the change. Three votes for a rise, rather than for a cut, is the first time the balance has pointed that way in this cycle, and it lands with headline inflation re-accelerating while core and services inflation sit still. Any Gateshead forward-looking work carrying a rate path or a real-terms deflator is now resting on an assumption that has moved. There is no local CPI, so this is national context rather than a Gateshead figure, but it is context that changes our own outputs.
What should we do?
Restate the rate and inflation assumption in the forecasting and cost-of-living work rather than carrying the July position forward silently. One paragraph in the next brief covers it. No dashboard work is needed.
16, 17 and 18 September · ONS and HM Land Registry

AmberThe North East Leads England on Both Rent and House Price Inflation

What changed?
Average UK monthly private rent rose by 3.8% to £1,400 in the 12 months to August 2026, with England at £1,459 and 4.0%. The North East and the North West had the highest rent annual inflation rate of all English regions, both at 5.8%. On prices, the North East was the English region with the highest house price inflation at 4.9%, against 1.1% for England (ONS private rent and house prices, published 16 September 2026). Gateshead's UK House Price Index average price for July 2026 is £155,550, an annual change of 3.7% (UK House Price Index, published 18 September 2026). The ONS also published median house price and sales volume for England and Wales for 2025 on 17 September, which goes down to small-area geography.
Why it matters?
Three housing releases at local authority level or finer inside four days is unusual, and they point the same way. The region with the lowest price base is running the fastest price and rent inflation in England. For Gateshead that is an affordability question rather than a wealth one, because rents are paid out of local wages and our wage position is weak. The small-area median price release is the piece that makes a borough answer possible, because it gives the geography to say where the squeeze is concentrated rather than only that it exists.
What should we do?
Scope a housing affordability deep dive built on the three together, answering whether Gateshead rent inflation is outpacing price inflation and where in the borough. This is a proposal rather than a commitment, and it would need commissioning. The small-area 2025 tables have not been pulled yet and that is the first step if it goes ahead.
17 and 18 September · DfE

AmberThe 14 Plus Reform Programme Now Has a Co-Design Forum, and It Seats Mayors

What changed?
The Education Secretary convened leaders from education, industry and local government at No.10 North on vocational pathways from age 14, work experience, V levels from 16, expanded T levels and devolved 16 to 19 funding, described as the first of regular gatherings (DfE, surfaced 18 September 2026). DfE also updated the Post-16 pathways implementation plan on 17 September with new content development milestones, changes to planned subjects and removals from the funded qualifications list. Separately, DfE published destinations data for key stage 4 and 16 to 19 students on 18 September.
Why it matters?
Until now this programme was an announcement with no named process. There is now a forum, and the seats belong to mayors rather than constituent councils, so Gateshead's influence on 14 plus pathways and on devolved 16 to 19 funding runs through NEMSA. The 2028 rollout is too late for the 18-24 cohort in the claimant count item above. Devolved 16 to 19 funding is nearer term, and it touches the age band where our own evidence says Gateshead performs well, so a funding change there is a risk to something that currently works rather than a fix for something that does not. The destinations release is the cleanest official measure of that 16 to 17 strength.
What should we do?
Put the question of what devolved 16 to 19 funding would mean for Gateshead providers to NEMSA, and pull the destinations data to refresh the 16 to 17 position with a current vintage. The qualification removals list is worth checking against what Gateshead providers actually offer.

How this reconciles. The gov.uk search API returned 124 rows across five organisation queries. The week in one paragraph. The August claimant count rose to 5,100 and the 18 to 24 band accounts for all of the increase on the year, which is the first hard local number behind the youth exposure we have been describing since July. Wednesday brings the first official 2024 GVA figure for Gateshead and a revised back series, which will tell us how our own estimates performed and will date every GVA figure we currently publish. Thursday brings a new business counts vintage. The Jobs Guarantee goes national in November aimed at long-duration 18 to 24 claimants, which is the largest lever available on the problem the claimant count just described, and we hold neither the delivery partner name nor the eligible cohort size. Three members of the Monetary Policy Committee voted to raise rates with inflation back at 3.1%, so the rate assumption behind our forward-looking work has moved. The new ONS productivity method halves the measured post-2008 slowdown and starts changing published figures in November. Two funding windows are open and one internal decision date has already passed. Coverage is the seven days since the last edition. Next edition: Monday 28 September.
