What's New · weekly round-up · covering 20–28 July

What's New — 28 July 2026

A short weekly read of what's coming, what's landed and what matters for Gateshead's economy. Each item says why it's here. This is the Monday 27 July edition, published a day late and widened to cover the full week and a half since the last one — and it is not a quiet edition. The month's biggest data drop landed on 21 July, including a new Annual Population Survey window, and the department that owns local growth policy changed hands on 22 July.

Two things need a decision this week. The Northern Growth Strategy views window and the Productivity Institute's call for papers both close on Friday 31 July. Both are set out under Live deadlines below.

What landed — data

Tue 21 July · ONS · the important one

UK Labour Market, July 2026 — and a new Annual Population Survey window

Why it matters: two Gateshead-level series refreshed on NOMIS at 07:00 that morning. The claimant count now runs to June 2026 (published as provisional and subject to revision), available down to ward and LSOA. More significantly, the Annual Population Survey has moved to the April 2025 – March 2026 window, superseding January–December 2025. That is the spine of our employment, unemployment, inactivity and qualifications figures, and it is the vintage the Quarterly Economy Monitor must be built on.

One caution carried into the monitor: a new APS window overlapping the old one is not an independent observation, so any movement is read against the confidence interval rather than treated as a change.

Tue 21 July · ONS

PAYE Real Time Information — no local authority update this month

Why it matters: worth knowing so nobody goes looking. The RTI local authority sheet is on a quarterly cycle and did not refresh; the next local update is 15 September. For a monthly employee proxy in the meantime the usable geography is Tyneside, which covers Gateshead alongside Newcastle, North Tyneside and South Tyneside — a four-borough figure, and it should always be labelled as one.

Wed 22 July · MHCLG · easy to miss

National non-domestic rates collected by councils in England, 2025-26

Why it matters: this one is genuinely useful and does not come through the usual ONS channel. It gives business rates collected and reliefs granted at local authority level — a Gateshead-specific administrative read on the business base, and the reliefs data speaks directly to the high streets work. Provisional, pre-audit. A natural angle for a future brief: retail and hospitality relief uptake in Gateshead against North East comparators.

Wed 22 July · ONS & HM Land Registry

Private rent and house prices (July 2026) and the UK House Price Index (May 2026)

Why it matters: both carry local authority detail, so both refresh the housing-cost side of the cost-of-living picture. Reference periods inside the ONS bulletin need checking before anything is quoted from it.

Wed 22 July · ONS

Consumer price inflation, June 2026

Why it matters: the deflator behind any real-terms statement we make, and the price basis for the July monitor. National only, no local breakdown.

Mon 20 July · ONS · to read before citing

Artificial intelligence in UK businesses, 2023 to 2026

Why it matters: directly relevant to the AI capability objective and to the North East AI pilot window in the NEET work — if it breaks below UK level. The release page does not say, so this needs opening before it is used. Flagged as a read, not a finding.

The week ahead — data

Wed 29 July · ONS · confirmed 09:30

Population estimates for England and Wales, mid-2025 — local authority, age and sex

Why it matters: a new latest population vintage landing the day before the Quarterly Economy Monitor. The recommendation is to quote mid-2024 throughout the monitor and label it, with a forward note that mid-2025 publishes on the 29th and is picked up next edition. The trap to avoid is recomputing rates on mid-2025 denominators this week: NOMIS's own denominators will not have moved, so the monitor would end up disagreeing with the source it cites.

Thu 30 July · ONS

Health state life expectancy for MSOAs, England and Wales, 2019 to 2023

Why it matters: small-area health data inside the borough, which is the evidence base behind the health-related inactivity story. MSOA level, so genuinely hyperlocal.

Fri 31 July · ONS

Update on reinstating quarterly regional GDP estimates for England and Wales

Why it matters: a methodology note, but the one to watch — it signals whether timelier sub-national output data is actually coming. We currently wait years for local GVA.

August & beyond · dates firming up

The autumn data calendar

Why it matters: three dates now shape the autumn work programme. Regional GDHI to 2024 is provisionally August 2026 with no confirmed day. Local authority GVA for 2024 is provisionally September 2026 — and the dataset page still says "to be announced", so it should be described as provisional, not scheduled. Subregional productivity for 2024 is confirmed for 5 November 2026, now stated on two independent ONS pages. All three carry back-series revisions, so each is a rebuild rather than an append.

Also dated: the next labour market release on 18 August, the UK business counts refresh on 24 September, and the LFS-to-TLFS position — ONS now aims to transition headline statistics during 2027, with no month specified and the next progress update due in August.

What changed — policy

Wed 22 July · Cabinet Office · the big one

Machinery of government: local growth policy moves to No.10 North

Why it matters: the department Gateshead writes to about growth has changed. Local economic growth and devolution strategy has moved out of MHCLG, and local economic growth policy out of HM Treasury, into No.10 North in Manchester, under the First Secretary of State. Anything currently in flight needs its addressee re-checked — and the criteria may follow the function, because a unit built around "good growth in every postcode" is likely to weight places differently from a Treasury-led one.

Two further changes matter to us. The revived National Economic Council seats regional mayors alongside Cabinet ministers, which is a new route to the centre — but it runs through the Mayor, not through constituent councils, so it raises the value of Gateshead evidence the Mayor can carry into that room. And DSIT has been abolished with its functions redistributed but not itemised, which leaves the sponsoring department for the North East AI Growth Zone currently unresolved. DBT is renamed BIST. DWP is unchanged, so the Get Britain Working and NEET architecture needs no re-basing.

Mon 27 July · Prime Minister's Office · announcement only

Technical education pathways from age 14 — direction of travel, not yet policy

Why it matters: aimed squarely at the NEET problem, which is a live workstream here. Confirmed: pupils from age 14 combining core academic subjects with technical education linked to local employers, modelled on the Greater Manchester Baccalaureate, with an ambition to begin rollout from September 2028, and Ofsted inspection changed to value technical quality. The sectors named are close to a one-for-one match with the North East LSIP employer priorities, so the region already holds the employer-led evidence a co-design would ask for.

Equally important is what is not established. This is a press release announcing an intention — no white paper, no consultation, no deadline and no new funding. Widely reported claims that technical qualifications will replace A-levels, and that it is funded by departmental reprioritisation, appear nowhere in the source and should not be repeated. The related Milburn review of young people and work is an interim report from May, not a new publication. And the 2028 start sits after the North East AI and skills pilot window we judge to be closing in early 2027 — so this is not a substitute for near-term action on the 18–24 cohort, which is where Gateshead is exposed.

Thu 23 July · Prime Minister's Office · legislation pending

Business rates cut for hospitality, VAT off domestic electricity, £2 bus fare cap

Why it matters: the 20% business rates cut for pubs, social clubs and live music venues from April 2027 is the most measurable of the three for us — it is a cost change to exactly the business types that anchor Felling, Winlaton and Chopwell. There is a baseline we do not currently hold and should: how many qualifying hospitality hereditaments sit in those three centres, established before it takes effect. VAT comes off domestic electricity from 1 October 2026, and the £2 bus fare cap returns in January 2027, changing the real cost of reaching Gateshead's centres. All three require legislation and no Budget date has been announced.

Live deadlines

Closes Fri 31 July · confirmed still open

Northern Growth Strategy — views window

Why it matters: final week, and three of its six question areas are ones this team holds evidence on — youth workforce participation, cultural institutions driving investment and regeneration, and business investment and access to finance. The strategy names Newcastle and Gateshead specifically. One risk worth stating in any response: this was a Treasury and MHCLG product, and that function has just moved to No.10 North, so the promised autumn update may not resemble what was consulted on.

Closes Fri 31 July

Productivity Institute academic conference — call for papers

Why it matters: the conference is on 14 September at Alliance Manchester Business School, one of its five themes is Place, travel is covered for presenting authors, and presenters are also invited to a policy conference with the Industrial Strategy Advisory Council the following day. The panel regression work behind the Local Economic Assessment is the obvious candidate. Decisions come back on 7 August. A draft paper or extended abstract is enough to submit.

Closes Wed 5 August

MHCLG — permitted development rights consultation

Why it matters: asset reuse and high-street conversion sit inside our placemaking objectives. Lower priority than the two above, but genuinely on-theme.

Filtered out this week

All tracked, none actioned.
