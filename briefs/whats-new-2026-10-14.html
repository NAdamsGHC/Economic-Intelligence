What's New, 14 October 2026

A short read of what’s landed, what’s coming, and what matters for Gateshead’s economy. This is a catch-up edition covering the thirteen days since 2 October. The 2 October edition replaced the one due on 5 October and this one replaces 12 October, so the departmental log is longer than usual. Three things carry it. NEMSA will control its Local Innovation Partnerships funding from 2028, and more on the new Good Growth Funds is promised at the Budget on 28 October. North East business activity rose in September, and the North of England led England’s regions for permanent hiring. And the Pathways to Work Innovation Fund closes in twelve days, with no partner from the NEMSA area in its directory.

What Landed

6 and 13 October · DWP · carried from 28 September

RedThe Innovation Fund Closes on 26 October, and the Partnership Directory Names No Partner From the NEMSA Area

What changed?
DWP added the partnership directory to the applicant bid pack on 6 October 2026, a day after the date the pack gave, and updated it on 13 October. It lists 245 entries, 230 of them organisations. The directory has no location field, and none of the entries names any of the seven NEMSA authorities or a place within them. The nearest are two Tees Valley entries, also in the North East region, a joint entry from Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council and Teesside University and one from First Stop Darlington. The page still says an online application portal will launch in early October, and applications can go by post or email in the meantime. Applications close on 26 October and shortlisting outcomes follow on 9 November.
Why it matters?
Economic inactivity driven by long-term sickness is one of the structural weaknesses in Gateshead’s labour market, and this money is aimed at it. Twelve days remain, and the item has carried since 28 September with no decision recorded.
What should we do?
Settle this week whether Gateshead bids, joins a bid or passes. The two Tees Valley entries are the nearest potential partners if Gateshead wants to join a bid. If Gateshead is involved, the evidence case on inactivity and health is ours to supply, and the ONS waiting times analysis in this edition adds to it.

The how to apply guidance was updated on 7 October 2026 to make clear that councils can submit one proposal in each application window and that the maximum for an individual proposal is £300,000. 2 and 12 October · BIST · carried from 2 October

RedElectricity Cost Scheme Registrations Close on 30 November

What changed?
The Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST) changed its business guidance twice. On 2 October it clarified that the eligible product codes are read against version 1.31 of the UK Tariff, dated 3 March 2026, and on 12 October it replaced the Annex A PDF with a web page. The eligibility list is still described as final for 2027. Applications close at 11:59pm on 30 November.
Why it matters?
Neither change alters who is eligible. Only firms applying this year can get the additional lump sum worth around a year’s relief, and BIST’s impact assessment estimates that small and micro businesses are 98% of businesses in the frontier sectors and 99% in the foundational ones (BIST, 1 October 2026).
What should we do?
Check that the scheme has reached business support colleagues. A count of potentially eligible Gateshead firms is still a proposal for commissioning.

9 October · No.10

AmberNEMSA Will Control Its Local Innovation Partnerships Funding From 2028

What changed?
The Prime Minister announced on 9 October 2026 that the existing Local Innovation Partnerships Fund will be devolved to established mayors from 2028, the first time mayors receive dedicated research and development funding (No.10, alongside his Innovation Nation speech). The North East is one of the Fund’s regions, each backed by at least £30 million (DSIT, 29 July 2025), and Homes England lists it among the Established Mayoral Strategic Authority areas (SAHP strategic priorities, November 2025, under the older North East Combined Authority name). DSIT had already said on 1 June 2026, naming the North East, that mayors would get this control after the next Spending Review, so the 2028 date is what is new. The announcement also commits £1 billion through UK Research and Innovation over four years to high-growth clusters in the Industrial Strategy’s priority sectors, and asks No.10 North to link clusters, starting with the Northern Growth Corridor and the Oxford to Cambridge Growth Corridor. Separately, a £40 million network of Robotics Adoption Hubs started on 9 October, with hubs running from Newcastle and the South West to Milton Keynes and South Wales. The release gives no detail of the Newcastle hub.
Why it matters?
Innovation money decided in the region is a lever NEMSA has not held before. The announcement places it next to the new Good Growth Funds, for which no detail is published, and the Prime Minister said more on the Good Growth Funds would be set out at the Budget on 28 October.
What should we do?
Watch the Budget for the size and rules of the Good Growth Funds. Ask NEMSA whether it will report where devolved innovation money is spent by local authority, so we can track what reaches Gateshead.
5 to 14 October · NatWest, KPMG and REC, S&P Global

AmberNorth East Business Activity Rose in September, and the North Led England for Permanent Hiring

What changed?
The North East’s NatWest Business Activity Index for September 2026 is 52.0, a renewed increase in output (NatWest UK Regional Growth Tracker, 14 October 2026). It is a diffusion index from a business survey, so a reading above 50 signals growth on the month and shows the direction and breadth of change, not a measured rate. No confidence interval is published. Ten of the 12 UK nations and regions grew, and input cost inflation picked up almost everywhere. The KPMG and REC Report on Jobs for the North of England, which covers the North East, the North West and Yorkshire and The Humber, found permanent placements rising at the strongest pace in 52 months and faster than in the other three English regions, with starting salary inflation at a 26-month high (8 October 2026). Across the UK, permanent placements rose at a pace that, though marginal, was the strongest in four years, and vacancies fell at the weakest rate in just over two years (KPMG). The S&P Global UK Services PMI Business Activity Index for September 2026 is 52.1, down from 52.5 in August (S&P Global, 5 October 2026). The construction release of 6 October could not be retrieved from source.
Why it matters?
These monthly surveys give the earliest read on the North East’s private sector, and they point the same way, towards firmer activity and hiring with rising costs. The regional surveys rest on small samples and cannot be broken down below the region. The official local read follows on 20 October with the September claimant count.
What should we do?
Use them in the Quarterly Economic Update on 29 October, paired with the claimant count and labelled as surveys.

9 and 12 October · ONS, DHSC

GreenLonger NHS Waits Are Associated With More Time on Benefits

What changed?
ONS analysis published on 12 October 2026 finds that, among working-age people in England receiving elective NHS treatment, treatment was associated with a reduction in benefits receipt for up to five years (ONS, The impact of elective waiting times on benefits receipt, England: April 2014 to April 2024). Compared with waiting times as they stood in 2015 to 2016, the last time the 18-week target was met, longer waits were associated with an estimated 38,972 to 60,572 additional person-years of benefit receipt over five years for inpatients, and 38,396 to 65,598 for outpatients. The largest increases were in trauma and orthopaedics for inpatients and neurology for outpatients. On 9 October the Department of Health and Social Care published the final report of the independent review into mental health conditions, ADHD and autism, covering prevalence, diagnosis, waiting times and recommendations for reform.
Why it matters?
Health-related inactivity is one of the live threads in our work, and this is linked national evidence that treatment waits are associated with time out of work. The figures are for England and the analysis gives no local breakdown. We have not yet read the DHSC review.
What should we do?
A full read of the DHSC review for the inactivity work is a proposal for commissioning. The ONS figures can support the Innovation Fund evidence case if Gateshead bids.

6 and 13 October · ONS

GreenONS Publishes the Component Approach Tables and New Industry Concentration Statistics

What changed?
On 6 October the ONS published the reference tables for the component approach to labour productivity, UK: 1997 to 2024, with productivity on both the existing and the new basis and the labour inputs behind them. On 13 October it published Trends in UK industry concentration: 2007 to 2025, which are official statistics in development. Across the UK non-financial business economy, the turnover-weighted average share of turnover held by the five largest firms in each four-digit industry fell from 0.45 in 2010 to 0.38 in 2025.
Why it matters?
The component approach changes the hours used to measure output per hour. It reaches UK figures first and local estimates later, so these tables show how far the national series moves before ours does. The concentration statistics are UK-level only.
What should we do?
Nothing now. Keep the old and new bases apart in the productivity re-run after 5 November.

How this reconciles. The gov.uk search API returned 219 rows across six organisation queries for the four feeds. 18 of those were the same page returned under more than one query, leaving 201 distinct entries. 3 were chapter pages of the school census manual, which is listed once, so they came out, leaving 198. 8 were the same publications already in the 2 October log, so they came out, leaving 190. The mailroom digests carried 213 departmental feed items. 112 were dated inside the window, covering 107 distinct pages, and every one was already in the search result. The other 101 were dated before the window and arrived late in the weekly feeds of 3 October. All but one of those pages are already in an earlier log or in this one, and the one that was not, a DfE school notice of 1 October, is added here, giving 191 log rows. 19 of them are forthcoming-release announcements rather than things published in the window, which leaves 172 actual publications. The announcements are marked and kept, because they carry the dates the next figures land.

Why both sources. Neither source is complete alone. The business department publishes under both the Department for Business and Trade (DBT) and the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST) organisations, and both are queried. Both MHCLG organisation slugs are queried, the live one and the legacy one from the department’s previous name, because the legacy one still carries items. 40 rows are pages that sat in an earlier log and were updated again in this window, and they are kept because the change is new. Centre-of-government items are not a departmental feed.
