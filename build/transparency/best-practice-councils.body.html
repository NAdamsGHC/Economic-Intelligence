Best Practice in Local Authorities

21 October 2026

What changes when a council uses AI agents for analysis. It builds on our General Best Practice guide and follows the Local Government Association's A playbook for agentic AI in local government (9 October 2026).

Match Governance to Risk

The playbook sets out three tiers of rollout, starting with personal use. Anything shared beyond the person who made it belongs at least in the second tier, where ownership is clear, data sources are defined and boundaries are agreed before it goes wider. We publish, so we hold all our work to that tier.

  • Start with why. Each use of AI should answer a service need the council has named
  • Give every agent a named owner in the council who is accountable for its outputs
  • Agree up front which decisions stay with a person. Anything that affects a resident's entitlement or a decision about them should never be left to an agent

Protect Data

  • Use public data wherever it answers the question. This site uses nothing else
  • Before any council or personal data goes near an AI tool, involve information governance, complete a data protection impact assessment and confirm where the data is processed and stored
  • Apply disclosure control on small areas, so nothing published could identify a person or a business
  • Check each tool's access through the council's cyber security and access control processes

Hold to Public Sector Standards

  • Quality assure analysis to HM Treasury's Aqua Book, and hold any cause-and-effect claim to the Magenta Book
  • Present statistics honestly, with their uncertainty shown, in the spirit of the Code of Practice for Statistics
  • Keep member-facing material politically neutral, describing policy by its content and date, with the same facts for every group
  • Meet the public sector accessibility regulations on anything published, including alt text and charts that do not rely on colour alone
  • Consider recording each tool under the government's Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard, which is mandatory for central government departments and recommended for the wider public sector

Be Open About AI Use

  • Publish a plain statement of how AI is used, who is accountable and what checks apply (our Transparency Statement)
  • Say clearly where AI is used, and that AI can be wrong (our note on the front page of the site does this)
  • Correct published errors in the open, and keep a public log of them

Look After People and Continuity

  • Train staff to direct and check AI work as well as to use it
  • Keep a runbook for every regular product, so service continues if a tool fails or a supplier changes
  • Run starters, movers and leavers processes for agent access, the same as for staff accounts
  • Run procurement and contract checks before any paid tool or data is used, covering data location, retention and exit

Things We Should Keep Doing

  • Keep this site on public data only
  • Talk to the council's information governance and digital teams before any of this approach is used on council data
  • Keep the Transparency Statement current as the team, the tools or the guidance change
