# Analytical assurance note — ELS scorecard, GDHI, productivity and Local Outcomes Framework dashboards

**Status: experimental analytical products, not official statistics.** Built from published official statistics, but the derived measures (ranks, quartiles, indices, better/worse directions) are the author's analytical judgements. Treat with caution and verify figures against the source releases before using them to inform decisions.

This note applies the **Aqua Book (2025)** framework — proportionate quality assurance, verification vs validation, an assumptions log, and named roles — to:

- [`economy/els-scorecard.html`](economy/els-scorecard.html) — all 88 ONS Explore Local Statistics indicators covering Gateshead
- [`economy/gdhi.html`](economy/gdhi.html) — GDHI per head benchmarking, 1997–2023, and the small-area GDHI explorer, 2010–2023 (the per-head benchmarking previously sat in a separate `gdhi-per-head.html`, merged into this product in July 2026)
- [`economy/productivity.html`](economy/productivity.html) — GVA and productivity position, 1998–2023, with the statistical-neighbour scorecard and UK league table
- [`performance-outcomes/lgof-comparator.html`](performance-outcomes/lgof-comparator.html) — Gateshead's position on MHCLG's Local Outcomes Framework (first edition, 1 July 2026), 112 live metrics rendered as 137 series against six comparator groups (added 30 July 2026)

Last updated: 30 July 2026.

---

## 1. Purpose, use and proportionality

These are **descriptive monitoring and benchmarking products**: they answer *"where does Gateshead stand, against whom, on what definition?"* They make no causal claims, no forecasts, and no appraisal of interventions.

**Assurance tier** (Aqua Book proportionality): *dashboard / published brief* — definitions block, caveats section, manifest provenance, structured review, and (this document) an assumptions log.

**Not sufficient for:** business cases, funding bids or investment decisions. Under the **Green Book (2026)**, evidence feeding an options appraisal needs a step-up this work has not had: a named **independent assurer**, sensitivity analysis of key assumptions, optimism-bias treatment, and additionality discipline (deadweight, displacement, substitution, leakage) applied to any claimed benefits. If a figure from these dashboards is carried into a business case, re-assure it at that tier first.

### NOMIS Quick Look (added 28 August 2026)

A reference-lookup product, not an analytical one: it re-presents published NOMIS figures for Gateshead and derives nothing. Proportionality is therefore set at the lowest tier — the risk it carries is a *stale or mislabelled figure being quoted onward*, not a wrong inference, and the assurance is aimed squarely at that.

- **Snapshot, not a live feed.** The data is embedded and was pulled on 28 August 2026. This is a deliberate departure from the alternative of live in-browser querying, which was tested and works (NOMIS returns `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`), but would have made the product the only one in the repo that breaks when its source is unavailable. The mitigation for staleness is that the build records, for every dataset, the newest period NOMIS holds at build time — read from each dataset's own `time.def.sdmx.json` — and the page states on each figure whether the snapshot is that vintage. A monthly calendar sweep re-runs the pull.
- **No analysis is performed.** Every figure is as published by ONS through NOMIS and is never re-derived. The only arithmetic the product does is to add up a breakdown's own rows, which is displayed alongside the published total rather than substituted for it, and a share-of-rows percentage whose denominator is labelled “% of these rows” — never presented as a share of the published total, which would be wrong wherever disclosure rounding makes the two differ. The page says so on its face rather than claiming nothing is calculated.
- **Rounding gaps are stated, never smoothed.** Where a breakdown's rows do not sum to the published total, the note gives both numbers and the size of the gap, and tells the reader to quote one or the other but not both in one sentence. This applies to BRES sections (93,130 against 95,000 for 2024), the claimant count by age (5,030 against 5,035 for July 2026) and UK Business Counts by legal status (5,575 against 5,580 for 2025). The sentence is generated from the actual figures, so it cannot fall out of step with them.
- **Basis is named where two exist.** UK Business Counts (`NM_142_1`, enterprises) is used and named as such, with the ONS Business Demography alternative and the local-units sibling (`NM_141_1`) declared on the face of the product. BRES is shown on the employees basis with the wider employment basis displayed beside it and the difference explained.
- **Where an interval is published it is drawn; where none is published none is drawn, and the product says which is which.** Of the six datasets only the Annual Population Survey publishes a confidence interval, and every APS estimate is shown with its own — see the next bullet. For the five count datasets the absence is an absence of published uncertainty, not of uncertainty: the claimant count, UK Business Counts and the mid-year population estimate are administrative, register-based or demographic counts with no sampling error to report; BRES is a survey whose NOMIS open-access release carries no interval at local authority level; and jobs density inherits survey error through BRES and through APS self-employment. The page states this rather than leaving the absence unexplained, and the sentence is generated from the registry, so adding a survey dataset rewrites it rather than leaving a stale claim.
- **Confidence intervals are drawn, not merely stored (added 28 August 2026 with the Annual Population Survey).** APS is the first survey dataset in this product and the first to publish an interval. Every APS estimate is shown with its 95% interval in five places: under the figure on the card, as its own column in the table, as error bars on the charts, as a shaded band on the time series, and inside the copied citation. The registry's `has_ci` flag drives this, and the gate fails the build if a dataset declares an interval that no chart carries. Where ONS suppresses the interval on an individual estimate it judges too small to be reliable - the RQF1 share, 1.7% for Jan 2025-Dec 2025 - that estimate is drawn without an error bar and the panel note states how many such estimates it contains.
- **A panel may carry an older period than its dataset headline.** APS qualifications are published only on the January-December period, so `date=latest` returns a rolling quarter with every cell empty, and which offset holds the populated period rotates each quarter. The pull asks for a five-period window and the build takes the most recent period that actually has data; the panel then shows its own period on its face as an amber chip, and its citations carry that period rather than the headline's. This is deliberate: the alternative was either a blank panel or a figure silently labelled with a date it does not have.
- **A breakdown row may be a measure in its own right, not a slice of one.** The count datasets split a published total into categories, so a row cites as "<measure>, <category>". APS rows are separate survey variables, so citing them that way produced "Gateshead's employment rate ..., Economic activity rate - aged 16-64, ... is 77.6%", which is self-contradictory. Breakdowns now declare which of the two they are, and APS rows cite under their own variable name qualified by the source.
- **Gateshead only.** No comparators and no ward breakdowns, by design: comparison belongs in the analytical products where a difference can be significance-tested rather than merely displayed.

- **Two defects found and fixed during the build.** The households-with-children-in-temporary-accommodation rate used NOMIS `NM_2026_1` as its denominator, which is *TS006 Population density*, not TS041 Number of Households: Gateshead read 1,378 households against the published 88,999, inflating the rate roughly 67-fold and giving a national median of 104 per 1,000 households — a tenth of every household in England. ## 5. Code of Practice for Statistics

Applied voluntarily: definitions and methods published (About tabs + this note); estimates labelled honestly (CIs shown, "official statistics in development" respected for APS-based measures, experimental status declared); revisions explained (back-series revision warnings on regional accounts); sources cited with publication dates on every indicator.

## 6. Known limitations and recommended step-ups

1. **No independent assurer yet** — the single biggest gap against the Aqua Book for anything beyond internal monitoring use.
2. No sensitivity analysis of the polarity classification (e.g. how the quartile-mix chart moves if contested directions flip). Cheap to add if the scorecard starts driving decisions.
3. Amenity per-head rates structurally favour small rural areas; cross-boundary use (Newcastle's amenities) is invisible at LAD level.
4. CI bands are latest-period only; trend charts show point estimates.
5. If any figure feeds a Green Book business case: independent assurance, sensitivity analysis, optimism bias and additionality treatment first (§1).
