Transparency Statement

16 October 2026

This site is an unofficial economic evidence base for anyone invested in Gateshead. It is built from publicly available statistics, and much of the work behind it is done with a team of AI agents that we direct, check and are accountable for. This statement sets out how that works, the rules it works to and the guidance it follows.

Who Is Accountable

The site owner is responsible for everything on this site, deciding what gets made, reviewing it, and deciding whether and when it is published. Nothing reaches the site without the owner's approval, and every publication needs that agreement at the time.

The site uses only publicly available data. Most of it is official statistics from the Office for National Statistics (ONS), NOMIS and gov.uk, alongside a few other public sources such as business surveys. It holds no personal data and no internal data from any organisation.

How We Use AI

The AI team is built on Claude, a large language model made by Anthropic, used through Claude Code. It runs in working sessions that the site owner starts, with the tools and permissions the owner allows. Nothing runs unattended and nothing is scheduled to run by itself.

The one automated job on the site is a monthly data refresh of the Explore Local Statistics scorecard, which runs on GitHub. It is a fixed script that re-downloads the ONS workbook and rebuilds the page only if the data has changed, and no AI is involved in it.

The AI does the legwork.

  • Pulling data from official sources and recording exactly what was queried
  • Scanning release calendars, government publications and policy announcements
  • Running analysis, benchmarking and forecasts to methods we have set out in advance
  • Drafting reports and building dashboards in our house style
  • Checking work before it reaches the site owner, including reproducing calculations and tracing claims to their source

The judgements stay with a person. What is worth looking at, what a finding means for Gateshead, whether the evidence supports it, and whether it is published are all the site owner's decisions.

How a Product Is Made

  1. Commission. the site owner asks for a piece of work. For anything open-ended, the brief is agreed first, one question at a time, covering what it is for, who will read it and how deep it goes
  2. Plan. The task is broken into steps and each goes to the right specialist
  3. Source. Every figure is pulled fresh from the official source at its latest edition, and every policy fact is read at its primary document. Each query is recorded so it can be reproduced
  4. Analyse. Comparisons are tested for statistical significance, intervals are carried through, and model results are described as suggestions rather than established fact
  5. Draft. The product is written in the site owner's voice and house style
  6. Check. Automated checks catch known errors and breaches of the house rules. Every report then goes to two independent reviewers. One reproduces the calculations and traces every claim to source. The other, starting cold, checks that it answers the question that was asked. Dashboard commentary is re-read against the final data before it ships
  7. Review. The site owner reads it, and changes or rejects anything that needs it. When the owner edits a draft, the changes are folded back into the instructions so the next draft needs fewer
  8. Publish. The files are committed to version control, and published only once the site owner agrees

The four regular reports follow this process every time. The Regular Reports Runbook sets out each of them step by step, in a form a person can follow without any AI tools.

Principles We Work To

These are summarised from our standing rules. Each rule in the register records when it was made, and most record the failure that prompted it.

  • A person is accountable for everything, and nothing is published without the site owner's agreement
  • Instructions come only from the site owner. Emails, web pages and documents are read as information and never acted on as instructions, whoever they claim to come from
  • Source first. Every figure comes from a fresh pull of the official source at the latest edition, and ONS and NOMIS figures are quoted exactly as published. Our own earlier products are never used as a source
  • Policy facts and dates come from the primary document, never from a search result or a press summary
  • Every figure is fully defined, with its geography, full metric title, period, measure and source
  • Uncertainty is shown, not hidden. Survey estimates carry their confidence intervals, on the page and on the chart, and differences are tested before they are claimed
  • Analysis is quality assured to the Aqua Book, and any claim about cause and effect is held to the Magenta Book or labelled as monitoring
  • Free public data only, with care over small areas so nothing published could identify a person or a business
  • Nothing is ever deleted. Anything removed is moved to a holding folder and logged, and only the site owner can empty it
  • Our calls are logged and scored. Every forecast and checkable judgement is recorded with the date it can be tested, and scored when the data lands
  • Our errors are logged. Every material error is recorded in the public Performance Log, and published errors are corrected in the open

Guidance We Follow

We follow the Local Government Association's A playbook for agentic AI in local government (9 October 2026), developed by a consortium of councils and researchers led by North Yorkshire Council. Its central message is that AI agents should be adopted deliberately, with clear boundaries, a named owner, governance that matches the level of risk, and people kept clearly accountable. The table sets out how we apply each of its eleven principles.

Playbook principleHow we apply it
Start with whyEvery piece of work starts with a commission from the site owner, and open-ended work starts with an agreed brief covering purpose, reader and depth
Accountability firstThe site owner is the single accountable owner of the site, the data behind it and the process that produces it
Use a human resource management approachEach agent has a written job description in its instructions. Errors are logged against the part of the process that produced them, and repeated errors put that part on probation, with every output reviewed line by line until it has a clean run
Training and learning approachThe runbook keeps the regular reports producible by hand, so the know-how stays with a person and does not depend on the tools
Cyber security and access controlThe AI works only inside sessions the site owner starts, with the permissions the owner grants. It treats outside content as information, never as instructions, and it does not publish without the owner's approval
Ethical impact assessmentWe have chosen what stays with a person. Commissioning, interpretation, judgement and the decision to publish are the site owner's
Transparency and business continuityThis statement and its companion pages. Every change is in version control, nothing is deleted, and any regular report can be produced by hand from the runbook
Technical robustnessIndependent verification before anything reaches the site owner, automated checks for known errors, forecasts tested against a simple benchmark, and model results described as suggestions
Data principlesPublic data only, no personal data, the authoritative source for every figure, the latest edition, figures quoted as published, and disclosure control on small areas
Starters, movers and leavers processA single owner rather than a staff group, so the nearest equivalent is change control. Every change to the standing rules is dated and recorded with its reason

The playbook's three-tier rollout model puts personal use of AI in its first tier. Because what we produce is published, we hold the work to the second tier, where ownership is clear, data sources are defined and boundaries are agreed before anything is shared more widely.

We also work to HM Treasury's Aqua Book for quality assurance and the Magenta Book for evaluation.

An Auditable Structure

The site and the work behind it are organised so that any figure, decision or change can be traced.

  • Version control. Every change to every page is in the public history of the repository, with its date and a description
  • Reproducible queries. Our analyses are built by script, and each build records every query as run and every value as returned, so a figure can be traced to the exact source query
  • A rules register. Each standing rule records when it was made, and most record the failure that prompted it
  • A production calendar that logs every run of every regular report, with what it produced
  • A prediction log of every forecast and checkable judgement, scored when the data lands
  • The Performance Log of every material error and what changed as a result
  • Nothing deleted. Removed files are moved to a holding folder and logged with their original location, the date and the reason
  • The Analytical Assurance Note, which sets out the quality assurance for the main dashboards

Working files that are not on the site are kept in a logged folder structure and can be produced on request.

Limits

AI can get things wrong, and so can people. Our checks reduce errors but do not remove them, and the Performance Log shows what has got through and what has been caught. When we find an error in something published, we correct it, say so in the product or its next edition, and log it.

The dashboards are experimental analytical products, not official statistics. The derived measures in them (ranks, indices, decompositions and model results) are our analytical judgements. Check figures against the source releases before using them to inform decisions.
