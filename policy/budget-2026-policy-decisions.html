Budget 2026 Policy Decisions

Nathaniel Adams, Economic Intelligence Analyst · Published 30 October 2026

Full Budget documents available at: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-document

Context

The Budget was presented on 28 October 2026. Table 6.1 of the Budget lists 98 costed policy decisions, meaning each has a published estimate of its effect on government borrowing. Each is summarised below in one sentence. The numbers are the table's line numbers and the headings are the government's own wording from the table. The costings are in Table 6.1 and the Policy Costings document. Figures in the sentences are those stated in the Budget's text and can differ from the costing in the table. The main measures and what they mean for Gateshead residents are in our summary of Budget 2026.

Some announcements are not in that table, so they are not listed here. These include measures with no net increase to borrowing (debt relief for councils in severe financial distress and the landlord registration fee), tax and annually managed spending measures worth less than £10 million in every year, and announcements without a costing (such as asking Ofgem to abolish gas standing charges). The money lent or invested through the financial transactions in Table 6.2 is also left out, although the decisions behind it are listed here.

Breathing Space for Families and Businesses

Breathing Space for Families

  1. Households in council tax bands A to D in England will receive a £100 Household Energy Payment, delivered by local authorities from January 2027, alongside £100 million for councils to give targeted support to households hit hardest by energy bills
  2. VAT on domestic electricity, cut from 5% to 0% from 1 October 2026, will now stay at 0% until 1 July 2027, three months longer than first planned, and is expected to apply UK-wide from April 2027
  3. The temporary 5p-per-litre fuel duty cut is extended for a further six months to 1 July 2027, and the planned increase in fuel duty in line with inflation (the Retail Prices Index, RPI) is deferred to the same date
  4. Single bus fares in England outside London will be capped at £2 for one year from 1 January 2027, funded from savings in the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero's budget
  5. The temporary 55p a mile tax-free rate for the first 10,000 miles in a car or van (in place from 6 April 2026) becomes permanent, and the rate above 10,000 miles rises from 25p to 35p from 6 April 2027
  6. Self-employed fathers, partners and adopters in Great Britain, and others who do not qualify for statutory paternity or adoption pay but have enough work and earnings history, will receive a new Parental Allowance from April 2029
  7. Carer's Allowance will no longer stop outright when earnings pass the Weekly Earnings Limit, with a 100% taper on earnings above the limit (£1 of allowance lost for each £1 earned) planned for 2030-31 in England and Wales
  8. The Universal Credit surplus earnings threshold (above which extra earnings carry into the next month's claim) stays at £2,500 in Great Britain for a further year, until the end of March 2028
  9. The government will spend £5 million a year for three years to increase the number of books given to pre-school children
  10. Tariffs are suspended on 135 food products from 21 June 2026 and on a further 101 food products and kerosene heating oil from 26 November 2026, both until 31 December 2028, and on fertiliser from 26 November 2026 until 31 December 2027
  11. The planned cut in the annual cash ISA limit from £20,000 to £12,000 for under-65s from 6 April 2027 is cancelled, so the limit stays at £20,000 for all savers over 18

Breathing Space for Businesses

  1. Business rates relief for pubs, social clubs and all but the very largest live music venues in England rises from 15% to 35% from 1 April 2027, with venues of rateable value (a property's assessed rental value) £1 million or more staying on the previously announced support
  2. Small Business Rates Relief thresholds for retail, hospitality and leisure properties in England rise from 1 April 2027, from a rateable value of £12,000 to £18,000 for 100% relief and from £15,000 to £21,000 for tapered relief
  3. From 1 April 2027, business rates bill increases for qualifying retail, hospitality and leisure properties in England are capped at inflation (the Consumer Prices Index) for two years, the 2026 Supporting Small Business scheme is extended for a year to those who had 2023 support, and the 1p Transitional Relief Supplement stays for two more years to help pay for this
  4. The income tax Trading Allowance rises from £1,000 to £3,000 a year from 6 April 2027
  5. Tariffs are suspended on 72 products applied for by businesses from 5 August 2026 until 31 December 2028, and suspensions on 361 products that were due to expire are extended
  6. From April 2028 the Climate Change Levy main rates for electricity and liquefied petroleum gas are frozen at 2027-28 levels, while the rates for gas and solid fuels rise with RPI inflation
  7. The temporary 3.7p-per-litre cut to red diesel duty is extended for six months to 1 July 2027, after which red diesel duty returns to its pre-March 2022 rate and is uprated by RPI inflation
  8. Small Producer Relief on alcohol duty becomes more generous from 1 February 2027, with a better discount for products under 3.5% alcohol, worth £10 million a year to over 2,000 small producers, while main alcohol duty rates rise with RPI inflation

Good Growth in Every Postcode

Delivering Fiscal Devolution

  1. Mayors and local leaders in England get £285 million in 2027-28 to set up Good Growth Funds, followed by a £1.5 billion borrowing facility for Mayoral Strategic Authorities (areas led by a mayor) from April 2028 that includes subsidised lending
  2. From April 2027 mayors will be able to receive a share of business rates income, in exchange for Investment Funds, Mayoral Capacity Funding and Local Growth Funds, plus a permanent 20% share of business rates growth in their areas, subject to consultation and without affecting constituent councils' core funding
  3. Mayoral Apprenticeship Brokerage Pilots expand from 7 to 14 Mayoral Strategic Authorities across England, with £100 million across 2027-28 and 2028-29, so that every current mayor can offer local apprenticeship support
  4. New funding goes to priority investments and local growth and regeneration projects across the UK, including a new £80 million Local Renewal Fund and named projects in Plymouth, Port Talbot, the Don Valley, Bristol and Leeds
  5. A pilot from April 2027 gives the Greater Cambridge Development Corporation direct access to government finance (repayable finance and a capital grant) for land, housing and infrastructure, with a possible extension to Oxford subject to consultation
  6. Mayoral Capacity Funding for strategic authorities rises by £150 million in 2027-28, with allocations to follow

Backing British Business

  1. A new Founders' Relief from 6 April 2027 gives qualifying founders of high-growth, innovative companies a 10% capital gains tax rate on up to £10 million of lifetime gains
  2. The R&D tax relief Claim Notification Form is removed from 1 April 2027 and the Additional Information Form is simplified from autumn 2027
  3. From 6 April 2027 the Company Share Option Plan (a tax-advantaged employee share scheme), renamed Enterprise Management Incentives 2, has its individual option limit raised from £60,000 to £120,000, its exercise period extended from 10 to 15 years and its interaction with the limit on the separate Enterprise Management Incentives scheme removed
  4. The British Business Bank brings £1 billion of equity commitments forward into 2026-27, offset by lower commitments later, and gets around £2 billion of extra financial capacity, part of a £5 billion uplift
  5. The National Wealth Fund gets an additional £15.4 billion of capital, including £2 billion for a new Energy Wealth Fund, taking its capitalisation from £27.8 billion to £43.2 billion
  6. A new package strengthens AI security, speeds up AI adoption and backs UK AI capabilities, including £120 million more for the AI Security Institute, £60 million more for the Sovereign AI Fund and a £60 million AI Pathfinder fund
  7. The government will spend £100 million over the next two years to reduce the cost of using local, lower-powered public electric vehicle chargers across Great Britain, from Spring 2027

Hope for the Future

Housing

  1. An additional £432 million across 2027-28 and 2028-29 takes total additional rough sleeping funding to £874 million from 2026-27 to 2028-29, with the aim of offering people sleeping rough in England a route off the streets
  2. Part of the extra rough sleeping funding comes from moving money within existing Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government budgets
  3. The Social and Affordable Homes Programme gets further capital funding for around 10,000 more homes in England, prioritised for Social Rent and especially council homes, allocated between 2027-28 and 2030-31
  4. Land sold to registered providers of social housing will be charged at a 0% rate of VAT from 1 April 2027
  5. A new £4.7 billion Your First Home scheme in England gives around 70,000 first-time buyers of new-build homes government equity loans of up to 20%, with a five-year interest-free period and the first loans from April 2027
  6. A new First Time Buyer ISA from April 2029 replaces the Lifetime ISA for new savers, paying a 25% government bonus on withdrawal towards a first home worth up to £450,000, with the bonus capped at £20,000
  7. From midnight on 29 October 2026 the Lifetime ISA withdrawal charge for first-time buyers purchasing above the £450,000 price cap falls from 25% to 20%

Investing in Our National Security

  1. On 30 June 2026 the government announced £15 billion for the Defence Investment Plan between 2026-27 and 2029-30, with £10.3 billion from reprioritised public spending, a commitment to further savings and a promise to confirm the full funding plan at the Budget
  2. The remaining £4.7 billion of the Defence Investment Plan is funded from capital budgets, through a further 1% cut to departmental capital budgets from 2027-28 to 2029-30 and £1.7 billion of unallocated Treasury capital contingency
  3. Approximately £190 million of defence and security administration savings is reinvested in frontline defence and national security from 2027-28 to 2029-30
  4. The UK will give an additional £500 million of military support this winter for Ukraine's air defences
  5. UK Export Finance capacity rises by £50 billion to support UK defence exports through guarantees and loans to overseas buyers, in place since June 2026

Funding the Budget Fairly and Responsibly

Modernising the Tax and Welfare System

  1. HMRC gets a package of measures to reduce the gap between tax due and tax paid, including more timely in-year payments for self-assessment and direct debits for VAT and Pay As You Earn liabilities from April 2029, direct recovery of lower value tax debts from the bank accounts of people and companies who can pay but repeatedly do not from July 2029, and 670 more compliance officers by 2029-30
  2. From 1 October 2027 online marketplaces must collect VAT on sales by UK businesses that are VAT-registered or sell £90,000 or more a year on the platform, excluding personal, second-hand and business-to-business sales
  3. The Department for Work and Pensions will use more data and targeted checks with the aim of stopping fraud entering the benefits system, including over 2,000 specialist fraud staff, capital checks in Pension Credit, checks on claimants living abroad and limits on repeat Universal Credit advances
  4. From 6 April 2027 people convicted of certain serious offences who are detained in hospital will no longer be entitled to benefits in Great Britain
  5. The tax treatment of company distributions (payments to shareholders) is reformed from April 2028 so it is consistent regardless of legal form, company residence or transaction structure
  6. Advance Corporation Tax legislation will be repealed on 1 April 2031, which removes any unused balances companies still hold
  7. From April 2027 overseas providers selling services to UK businesses that are not VAT-registered must charge and account for UK VAT themselves, and the reverse charge (where the customer accounts for the VAT) continues only for VAT-registered UK businesses
  8. A new Pension Tax Allowance from 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2030 means pensioners whose total income is no more than the full new State Pension do not pay small amounts of income tax
  9. Pension and lump sum death benefits paid on or after 28 October 2026 are no longer taxable solely because they are paid more than two years after the member's death
  10. A new statutory regime from 1 January 2029 brings certain real estate income and gains of foreign sovereign investors into UK tax

Making Fair Choices on Revenue Raising

  1. The bank corporation tax surcharge rises from 3% to 6% for 15 months, from 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2028
  2. Machine Games Duty rises from 20% to 25% on machines with stakes over £1 or prizes over £100 from 1 April 2027, and liability moves to machine suppliers for supplier-owned machines under profit-share arrangements from 1 April 2028
  3. From April 2027 adult gaming centres in England lose the lower retail, hospitality and leisure business rates multipliers (the pence in the pound applied to a property's value), and vape shops lose both those multipliers and Small Business Rates Relief
  4. Businesses can deduct 50% rather than 40% of main rate plant and machinery spending from taxable profits in the first year from 1 January 2027, and the yearly deduction on what remains (the main rate writing-down allowance) falls by 2 percentage points to 12% from April 2027
  5. From 2 January 2028 larger private hire vehicle operators become liable for 20% VAT on the full value of their fares, with £50 million annual fare turnover the lead option for the threshold
  6. The lifetime limit for Business Asset Disposal Relief (a lower capital gains tax rate on selling a business) falls from £1 million to £500,000 for disposals from 6 April 2027
  7. Income tax rates on property income rise by a further 2 percentage points from 6 April 2027, to 24% (basic), 44% (higher) and 49% (additional) in England, Wales and Northern Ireland
  8. Income tax rates on savings income rise by a further 2 percentage points from 6 April 2027, to 24% (basic), 44% (higher) and 49% (additional) across the UK

Transforming the Civil Service

  1. Government administration budgets will fall by 20% after inflation by 2029-30 compared with 2025-26, against a 16% cut in Spending Review 2025 plans
  2. £50 million of the administration savings is reinvested in a new Civil Service AI transformation fund across 2027-28 and 2028-29

Other Policy Measures Including Pre-announced Policies

Pre-announced Breathing Space Measures and Associated Funding

  1. Vehicle Excise Duty for most heavy goods vehicles was cut to £1 for licences taken out between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027
  2. VAT on children's meals, children's and family tickets to cinemas and performing arts, and admission to specified family attractions was cut from 20% to 5% from 25 June to 1 September 2026 under the Great British Summer Savings Scheme
  3. Bus travel was free for children aged 5 to 15 in England during August 2026
  4. The foreign permanent establishment exemption becomes mandatory for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, so losses of overseas branches no longer reduce UK corporation tax
  5. The Electricity Generator Levy rate rose from 45% to 55% from 1 July 2026 until 31 March 2028, and the levy will continue past 2028

Pre-announced Growth Measures

  1. From 1 April 2027 the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme exempts eligible businesses in Great Britain from the indirect costs of the Renewables Obligation, Feed in Tariffs and Capacity Market, with a backdated payment in 2027
  2. Carbon Price Support rates, a carbon price top-up on fossil fuels used by electricity generators, end in Great Britain from April 2028
  3. The Spring 2026 Youth Employment and Skills Package brings a £3,000 Youth Jobs Grant, extends the Jobs Guarantee to people aged 22 to 24 and adds a £2,000 apprenticeship hiring payment for employers in England who do not pay the Apprenticeship Levy
  4. The Union Learning Fund is re-established in England with £15 million a year from 2027-28 for union-led learning and training projects
  5. The government sets aside approximately £2.4 billion from 2026-27 to 2031-32 for strategic technologies, local growth and key industries, plus approximately £1.8 billion of loans and investment in Table 6.2, which the Budget documents do not itemise
  6. The British Business Bank's Growth Guarantee Scheme expands to support £6.5 billion of additional lending to small and medium-sized businesses across this Parliament
  7. Leeds City Council will keep 100% of business rates growth for 25 years in a new Leeds City Fund zone from 1 April 2027, subject to legislation
  8. The UK-India trade agreement and Double Contributions Convention came into force on 15 July 2026, cutting tariffs on goods imported from India
  9. From 1 July 2026 the quota of steel that can enter the UK tariff free was cut by 51% compared with the previous safeguard, and the out-of-quota tariff rose to 50%
  10. The Securities Transfer Tax replaces Stamp Duty and Stamp Duty Reserve Tax on shares from late 2027
  11. Eligible electric vehicle charge points and electric vehicle-only forecourts get 100% business rates relief for ten years from 1 April 2026

Priority Public Services Funding

  1. £15 million goes to grassroots misogyny prevention through the Office for the Impact Economy, which also supports the National Taskforce for Tackling Misogyny
  2. The Infected Blood Compensation Scheme implements recommendations of the Inquiry's Additional Report, through regulations in force from 24 July 2026
  3. Interest rates on Plan 2 and Plan 3 student loans are capped at 6% from 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027
  4. Landfill Tax costs are funded for six priority illegal waste site clearances until March 2029, and a three-year pilot from 2027 funds councils' Landfill Tax bills from clearing illegal waste sites in their areas
  5. Other priorities get extra funding, including £190 million for NHS winter pressures in England and faster delivery of the Violence Against Women and Girls Strategy

Other Measures

  1. Company car tax rates for 2030-31 are set 1 percentage point above 2029-30 across all bands, up to a maximum of 40%, taking zero-emission cars from 9% to 10%
  2. A licensing regime from 2027-28 will enforce national standards for supported housing in England
  3. Medicines donated in qualifying circumstances are charged at a 0% rate of VAT from 23 June 2026
  4. The VAT exemption for locum doctors supplied through employment agencies and similar organisations is removed from 1 May 2027, which the government says restores the intended position
  5. The cap on Landfill Tax credits for contributions to the Landfill Communities Fund rises from 5.3% to 8.3% of operators' Landfill Tax liability from 1 April 2027 in England and Northern Ireland
  6. From academic year 2028/29 franchised higher education providers in England with 300 or more students must register with the Office for Students for their courses to qualify for student finance
  7. Changes to the high needs budget, which funds support for children with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND), for 2026-27 and 2027-28 (extra funding for pay, less a reduction from the Teachers' Pension Scheme valuation) reduce the in-year SEND deficits councils build up, in those two years only
  8. The High Value Council Tax Surcharge, a new annual charge from April 2028 on owners of properties in England valued at £2 million and above, will have a deferral scheme, discounts and exemptions, and a 100% premium on properties left empty for 12 months or more
  9. Mandatory payrolling of company cars, vans, fuel and medical benefits is delayed from 6 April 2026 to 6 April 2027, with most other benefits in kind following from 6 April 2028
  10. Removal of the customs duty relief on imports worth £135 or less, with new customs arrangements, is brought forward from 1 March 2029 to October 2028 at the latest
  11. The expansion of the UK Emissions Trading Scheme to emissions from energy from waste and waste incineration is delayed (the Budget documents give no new start date)
  12. The one-off corporation tax impact of revenue recognition changes for qualifying gas distribution activities is spread over five years, for accounting periods ending on or after 31 December 2026
  13. UK-resident individuals who invest in US Limited Liability Companies and similar entities will get relief from double taxation from early 2028
