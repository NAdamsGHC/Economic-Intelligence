Policy update, November 2026

Sixth edition · 12 November 2026 · public, compiled from published official sources · scan covers 2 October to 12 November 2026 · replaces the 2 October 2026 edition (retained in policy/)

New and changed government and regional policy affecting Gateshead's economy. Findings are checked against the strategy and guidance documents we hold as current, listed with their status at the end. Sourced from gov.uk, North East Mayoral Strategic Authority (NEMSA) and parliament publications. Each item is graded by what it asks of us, and flagged Local, Digital or Visitor where it touches a Policy Advisor's objectives.

The month in one line. The Budget on 28 October wrote down the fiscal devolution mechanism, with mayors taking a 20% share of business rates from April 2027 and income tax from April 2028, and the detail now waits for the 2027-28 settlement and the Spending Review in 2027. Alongside it came a high streets strategy that names Gateshead's Old Town Hall, £1.91m for the borough from the Derelict Buildings Fund, and a High Street Rental Auctions Fund that opens the week of 23 November. The Visitor Levy Bill was introduced on 4 November, the Jobs Guarantee has a named North East delivery partner, and the Green Book discount rate fell to 3.0%.

1. Fiscal Devolution and the Centre

28 October · HM Treasury · Local

AmberThe Fiscal Devolution Mechanism Is Written Down, and the Detail Moves to 2027

What changed?

The Budget and the Fiscal Devolution Roadmap set out 'income tax assignment for all Mayors beginning in 2028; increased business rates retention, with Mayors retaining a 20% share of locally retained business rates from next year, and with local and regional government retaining 100% of business rates income by the end of the decade' (Budget 2026, para 4.60; Fiscal Devolution Roadmap, HM Treasury, 28 October 2026). Councils' own share rises 'to at least 70%', starting with mayoral strategic authorities receiving 'a 20% share of business rates from April 2027' (para 4.66). 'Fiscal devolution baselines will be set at the spending review in 2027' (para 4.60).

The North East is in the first wave of mayoral Business Rates Retention Zones, 'subject to Full Business Case approval and legislation' (para 4.61). The Roadmap says the new mayoral share 'will not affect constituent councils' core funding'. Income tax is assigned on where taxpayers live, starting with the basic rate, and its final parameters wait for the Spending Review. Further detail on business rates comes in the 2027-28 Local Government Finance Settlement.

Why it matters?

Assigned income tax goes to NEMSA to replace its grants, and nothing passes to constituent councils by design. The questions that decide the money (baselines, resets and how the mayoral share is spent) are now for 2027.

What should we do?

Nothing before the next edition. The 2027-28 provisional settlement is the next point to read.

2 October to 12 November · Cabinet Office, Prime Minister's Office and HM Treasury

GreenNo Devolution White Paper, and Everything Else From the Centre

What changed?

The Devolution White Paper was not published with the Budget. The Budget instead says 'The Rewiring the State Delivery Plan will be published before the end of the year' (para 4.58). The Cabinet Secretary's annual lecture on 10 November spoke of 'genuinely devolving power, not just delegating it', and said the Future Civil Service review will report to the Prime Minister before next summer. The 10 Year Plan and the Milburn Review's final report were not published in the window. The other policy items from the centre are folded away under Also Noted, at the end.

Why it matters?

The delivery plan is now the document that should say how devolution reaches constituent councils, if anything does.

What should we do?

Nothing for us this month.

2. High Streets, Place and the Visitor Economy

28 October · MHCLG · Visitor

RedHigh Street Rental Auctions Fund Opens the Week of 23 November

What changed?

The High Street Rental Auctions Fund 2026 to 2028 prospectus (MHCLG, updated 28 October 2026) offers up to £10m to local authorities with planning powers, for vacancy registers, market reports, property surveys and refurbishment. Refurbishment is limited to three premises per authority and capped at £200,000 a project. Applications open in the week commencing 23 November 2026 and close on 31 January 2028, or earlier if the money runs out. They are 'assessed on a rolling basis with early applications prioritised', and all funded work must finish by 31 March 2028.

Why it matters?

The fund pays for a vacancy register, which is the occupancy evidence the Visitor objectives need. Early applications are prioritised, so applying in the opening weeks matters.

What should we do?

Regeneration colleagues decide whether to apply, and if so, early.

28 October · MHCLG · Visitor, Local

AmberA High Streets Strategy Names Gateshead's Old Town Hall, and the Borough Gets £1.91m for Derelict Buildings

What changed?

Hope on the High Street (MHCLG, CP 1704, 28 October 2026) is the promised high streets strategy, with a '£210 million Community Assets Programme' (para 133). It includes £10m to speed up High Street Rental Auctions, a new Community Right to Buy with a £65m fund, a 'high streets first' principle for the public estate, and reform of Business Improvement Districts. MHCLG will track persistent vacancy, which the strategy puts above 8% in the North East. It names Gateshead directly, saying brownfield funding 'is supporting Capital and Centric's redevelopment of Gateshead's Old Town Hall and Civic Quarter' (para 168). Vape shops and adult gaming centres will need planning permission, subject to a consultation with no date yet.

The Derelict Buildings Fund methodology note (MHCLG, 28 October 2026) allocates £120m of the £125m fund across 60 councils, ranked on employment deprivation and shared by population 'subject to a £1 million floor and a £3 million cap'. Gateshead receives £1.91m. In the North East, County Durham receives £3.00m, Sunderland £2.71m and South Tyneside £1.43m. The interim guidance expects councils to begin community engagement and start to longlist buildings by the end of 2026, with a first progress update to MHCLG by April 2027 and detailed guidance on project selection and funding profiles to follow. The fund runs from 2026 to 2030.

Why it matters?

The £1.91m comes without a bid, so the open question is which buildings it goes to, and the longlist starts before the end of the year. The national vacancy measure will become the yardstick the borough's centres are compared against.

What should we do?

Regeneration colleagues, for the community engagement and longlist due by the end of 2026.

28 and 30 October · HM Treasury and MHCLG · Visitor, Local

AmberPubs Get 35% Rates Relief From April 2027, and the Multipliers Were Corrected Two Days After the Budget

What changed?

The Budget gives an 'extra 20% relief for pubs, social clubs and all but the very largest live music venues in England from 1 April 2027', bringing their total relief to 35% (para 3.21). For retail, hospitality and leisure (RHL) properties, small business rate relief thresholds rise 'to £18,000 for 100% relief (from £12,000) and £21,000 for tapered relief (from £15,000)', with a real-terms freeze on RHL bills in 2027-28 and 2028-29 (para 3.22). From April 2027 vape shops and adult gaming centres lose the RHL multipliers, and vape shops lose small business rate relief (para 3.25).

MHCLG's business rates information letter 8/2026 was updated on 30 October 'to ensure that inflation adjustments are consistently applied to the RHL multipliers'. The 2027-28 multipliers are 49.7p standard, 44.7p small business, 44.5p standard RHL, 39.5p small business RHL and 52.5p high value. The letter commits to New Burdens funding for 'the reasonable costs, borne by local authorities for implementing the business rates measures', and from April 2027 to reimbursing councils' reasonable external legal costs when they lose cases that test a point of law. It says nothing on compensation for lost rates income.

Why it matters?

Three changes from April 2027 land on the same high street premises, and lower bills mean lower retained rates income. The letter covers implementation and legal costs, so how lost income is compensated is not yet stated. Anyone quoting the RHL multipliers from Budget day should use the corrected figures.

What should we do?

Revenues colleagues will have the letter. Business support colleagues can tell pubs and venues what changes in April.

4 November · MHCLG · Visitor

AmberThe Visitor Levy Bill Is in Parliament

What changed?

The Visitor Levy (England) Bill was introduced in the Commons on 4 November (MHCLG, 4 November 2026). The levy is charged 'as a percentage rate of the cost of accommodation, rather than as a flat fee', and 'will only apply to the first 28 consecutive days of a stay'. In Foundation Strategic Authorities a levy needs unanimous support. Ten mayors have committed that any levy in their area would be no higher than 5%. The bill itself sets no cap. The North East Mayor is quoted supporting a levy. No second reading date is set (bill page), and revenue plans are expected by March 2028 (Fiscal Devolution Roadmap).

Why it matters?

The levy would be set and spent by NEMSA. The borough's influence comes through NEMSA's consultation, which has to happen before any levy starts.

What should we do?

Nothing until NEMSA consults.

13 October · NEMSA · Visitor, Local

AmberA Draft 25-Year Framework for the Newcastle Gateshead Urban Core

What changed?

NEMSA published a draft Strategic Regeneration Framework presenting 'a shared vision for the "urban core" of Newcastle and Gateshead over the next 25 years', including more than 3 million sq ft of additional commercial space, with Gateshead Quays named (NEMSA, 13 October 2026). The draft goes to the cabinets of Newcastle City Council, Gateshead Council and NEMSA before a public consultation. No dates are published. The Budget speech also cited 'the urban regeneration in Gateshead Quayside, led by the Mayor of the North East' as the kind of project the new Good Growth Funds support.

Why it matters?

It is the long-term frame for Gateshead Quays and the town centre, and the consultation will be the place to put the Gateshead evidence.

What should we do?

Respond when the consultation opens.

Updated 16 October · NEMSA · Visitor

RedNEMSA Events Fund Expressions of Interest Close on 26 November

What changed?

The Mayor's Events Fund (NEMSA, published 2 October, updated 16 October 2026) is an £11m three-year fund. Requests 'can range from £10,000 to £500,000' for 'events taking place between April 2027 and March 2029', with expressions of interest to eventsfund@northeast-ca.gov.uk by 26 November 2026. Shortlisted projects will be invited to develop a full business case. NEMSA's Small Venues Fund also says phase one applications open in November 2026, for grants of £3,000 to £100,000, with no closing date published.

Why it matters?

Events can bring visitor activity to places outside the established hotspots, which is the Visitor objective, and the window closes before the next edition.

What should we do?

Events, culture and venue colleagues, so organisers in the borough know.

AmberAn Investment Summit on Tyneside in Spring 2027, and Two Documents Disagree on Where

What changed?

The Budget speech says 'we will hold the next International Investment Summit in Gateshead in the Spring' (transcript, 'exactly as it was delivered'). The Budget document says it 'will take place in spring 2027 in Newcastle' (para 4.123). Neither has been corrected. Other investment and business support items from the Budget and since:

  • A £700m Strategic Sites Accelerator for 'commercially viable sites with strong investor demand', with the North East among the regions being explored and no submission route yet (BIST, 2 November 2026)

Either way the summit is on Tyneside, which makes it the main near-term moment to put Gateshead's sites in front of investors. The venue should not be quoted as settled until one of the documents is corrected.

Inward investment colleagues. Business support colleagues can pass Scale UK Connect to growing firms before January.

4. Labour Market and Skills

21 October · DWP

AmberThe Jobs Guarantee Has a North East Delivery Partner

What changed?

DWP added its preferred applicants for the national rollout, subject to signed grant agreements. For the 'North East and Tees Valley' it is Ingeus UK Ltd (Jobs Guarantee, DWP, updated 21 October 2026). The page still says the national rollout 'will go live later in 2026'. The guarantee is a paid six-month job for 18 to 24 year olds on Universal Credit and looking for work for 18 months.

Why it matters?

The guarantee is aimed at young people who have been looking for work for a long time, and employers will need to know who to approach in the North East once grant agreements are signed.

What should we do?

Employment support colleagues, ahead of go-live.

15 October to 11 November · DfE, DWP, BIST and the Prime Minister's Office

GreenA Welfare Response by the End of the Year, and More Room for Colleges

What changed?
  • The government will respond to the Milburn and Timms reviews 'before the end of this year', with 'A White Paper will follow early in 2027 ahead of legislation later that year' (Budget 2026, para 5.21).
  • Colleges get funding certainty for enrolling young people who are NEET, more flexibility on English and maths hours, and local leaders a greater role in shaping 16 to 19 provision (DfE, 11 November 2026)
  • Hospitality brands committed to 100,000 paid jobs over three years for NEET 18 to 24 year olds, with no regional breakdown (DWP, 15 October 2026)
  • Job adverts will have to 'say what you pay', with legislation 'as soon as possible' (5 November 2026)
  • Industrial Strategy Jobs Plans for construction, professional and business services, and digital and technologies (19 to 21 October), which give 'a common framework that Mayoral Strategic Authorities and local authorities can draw on alongside Local Skills Improvement Plans', and £100m over two years for mayoral apprenticeship services (Budget, para 5.22)
Why it matters?

16 to 19 provision keeps moving towards mayors. The hospitality commitment is an entry route for 18 to 24 year olds if the brands' Gateshead sites take part.

What should we do?

Nothing for us this month.

5. Procurement, Community Wealth Building and Appraisal

9 October to 2 November · Cabinet Office, MHCLG and HM Treasury · Local

AmberSocial Value Guidance Is Published, and the Statement That Binds Councils Comes in March 2027

What changed?
  • Guidance on PPN 026, the social value model, was added on 28 October. The PPN itself (5 August 2026) applies to central government only, for procurements started from 1 January 2027, with minimum social value weightings of 10% for contracts of £1m to £5m and 20% above
  • A new National Procurement Policy Statement, to be laid in March 2027, 'will prioritise delivery of high-quality jobs, fair pay and skills opportunities', and a UK supplier definition is due by January 2027 (Budget 2026, paras 4.115 and 4.116)
  • The Local Growth Plans guidance section on social value was rewritten on 9 October. Mayoral authorities and constituent councils should 'align social value statements or strategies to their Local Growth Plan'
  • A £10m Regional Co-operative Development Programme, with NEMSA among the eligible authorities and details 'in due course' (MHCLG, 28 October 2026), and a new Co-operatives and Mutuals Champion
Why it matters?

The central government model is a ready template for the council's own social value framework, and the March 2027 statement is the instrument that reaches council procurement. The co-operative programme is funded and routes through NEMSA.

What should we do?

Procurement colleagues, before the statement is laid in March 2027.

22 and 28 October · HM Treasury and the Prime Minister's Office · Visitor, Local

AmberThe Green Book Discount Rate Falls to 3.0%, With New Guidance on Place-Based Business Cases

What changed?

HM Treasury published a revised Green Book on 28 October, 'formalising the reduction in the discount rate from 3.5% to 3.0%' (Budget 2026, para 2.22), with updated supplementary guidance on discounting. New guidance on developing place-based business cases describes 'a new approach to appraisal that puts local objectives at the heart of decision making' (para 2.10).

Why it matters?

A lower discount rate gives more weight to benefits that arrive years later, which helps regeneration schemes. Place-based business cases are likely to be the format Derelict Buildings Fund and Good Growth Funds projects are judged in.

What should we do?

Anyone writing a business case should use the revised Green Book from now on.

6. AI, Digital and the Testbed Objective

20 October to 5 November · HM Treasury, BIST and the Cabinet Office · Digital

AmberLocal AI Partnerships Start in Yorkshire, and Data Centres Get a Policy Statement

What changed?
  • The Budget provides '£30 million to pilot local partnerships, starting with West Yorkshire Combined Authority, South Yorkshire Mayoral Combined Authority, and York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority', working across councils 'to help small businesses grow' (para 4.135)
  • A Data Centre Covenant expects strategic data centres to agree a Local Benefit Plan 'spent on local priorities, like transport, parks and town centres' (para 4.137). The draft National Policy Statement for Data Centres is out for consultation until 8 January 2027
  • The Digital and Technologies Jobs Plan (21 October) (Industrial Strategy Jobs Plans) lists RTC North as the North East TechFirst school and college delivery contact, and the AI adoption survey 2026 was published on 29 October
  • Who sponsors the North East AI Growth Zone after the science department's abolition is still not stated on any page
Why it matters?

The local AI partnership model (councils, small business adoption and skills together) is the testbed objective, and the North East is not in the first wave. Local Benefit Plans could apply to data centres at the AI Growth Zone sites near the borough.

What should we do?

Digital colleagues, ahead of any second wave and before the consultation closes on 8 January.
