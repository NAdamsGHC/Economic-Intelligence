Gateshead Economic Intelligence · Policy Update

Policy update, October 2026

Fifth edition · 2 October 2026 · public, compiled from published official sources · scan covers 8 September to 2 October 2026 · replaces the 8 September 2026 edition (retained in policy/)

New and changed government and regional policy affecting Gateshead's economy. Findings are checked against the strategy and guidance documents we hold as current, listed with their status at the end. Sourced from gov.uk, North East Mayoral Strategic Authority (NEMSA) and parliament.uk publications. This edition is the first built to the new format, so it opens with a one-page dashboard that grades each item by what it asks of us.

The month in one line. Devolution stopped being an announcement and started arriving as instruments, and most of them land with NEMSA rather than the borough. The visitor levy design was settled with no borough share, apprenticeship brokerage was extended to every mayoral authority, NEMSA launched a regional business support framework with a spoke in each council, and NEMSA was named to copy a shared occupational health scheme for small firms. Meanwhile Gateshead was shortlisted for Young Futures Hubs and not selected, while three of its NEMSA neighbours were and a fourth already has them. The fiscal mechanism itself is still unpublished, and everything about it now sits behind the Budget on 28 October.

1. Fiscal Devolution and the Centre

Budget 28 October · HM Treasury and NEMSA

RedThe Budget Carries the Whole Fiscal Question, and the Spending Review Is Now Placed in 2027

What changed?

Nothing was published on the mechanism. The Budget remains confirmed for Wednesday 28 October 2026, with an OBR Economic and Fiscal Outlook alongside (Chancellor's letter to the Treasury Select Committee, 31 July 2026). No percentage, baseline, reset period, equalisation formula or list of abolished grants has appeared in any document in the window. The devolution white paper is still referred to only as forthcoming. A Department for Education release of 10 September describes a summit held 'ahead of the publication of the Devolution white paper' and gives no date (DfE, 10 September 2026).

Two things did move. The September update on Keep Britain Working plans a phased roll-out 'ahead of the Spending Review in 2027' (DWP, 23 September 2026), which is the first published placing of the next Spending Review we have found. That matters because the Cabinet Statement says 'we will use the upcoming Spending Review to confirm the detailed arrangements for retained income tax' (Rewiring the State, 31 July 2026). And the Mayor wrote to the Chancellor on 11 September with three immediate priorities, being affordable local transport, greater powers over skills and post-16 education, and fiscal devolution, alongside a proposed £500 million North East Growth Fund (NEMSA, 24 September 2026). The Mayor said 'any new settlement must be fair'.

The Budget will also confirm Your First Home, a new-build equity loan scheme backed by 20% government equity loans with 'a household income cap with local property price caps', the detail of which 'will be set out at Budget' (MHCLG, 26 September 2026).

Why it matters?

Swapping a needs-based grant for a share of locally raised tax favours places with a strong tax base and low demand for services. Gateshead has neither, so the equalisation design decides whether this is a gain or a loss for the borough, and it is still undesigned two months after the announcement of 30 July. A Spending Review in 2027 means the income tax half of the answer could sit a year beyond the Budget.

The local price caps on Your First Home decide who it reaches here. A cap set on national or regional prices could sit well above typical Gateshead new-build prices.

What should we do?

Read the roadmap, the Budget documents and any white paper in full on 29 October, against the open questions on our watch list. The income tax base position the August and September editions said was outstanding was in fact derived in our internal fiscal devolution briefing of 31 July 2026, so it is ready to set against whatever basis the roadmap chooses. Read the Your First Home caps against Gateshead new-build prices the same day.

10 September · MHCLG and HM Treasury · Visitor economy

AmberThe Visitor Levy Is Designed, and None of the Decisions That Matter Sit With the Borough

What changed?

The government response to the visitor levy consultation was published on 10 September, the same day as a Mayoral Council at No.10 North (Visitor levy in England, response added 10 September 2026; MHCLG, 10 September 2026). The levy is charged 'as a percentage of the cost of accommodation, rather than as a flat fee'. Mayors and leaders of Foundation Strategic Authorities 'can decide whether to introduce a levy and how the revenue should be invested', and accommodation providers are liable for it. The response itself says strategic authorities 'will not be able to vary rates by season, accommodation type or constituent authority', and that decisions on the revenue are theirs, which 'could include providing funding to local authorities in their areas, where the strategic authority decides to do so' (government response, PDF). Strategic authorities must consult before a levy starts and then publish a final prospectus. Subject to Parliament, the government expects plans for how revenues will be invested 'by March 2028'.

Why it matters?

The levy is the first fiscal power to arrive, and it arrives at NEMSA. The borough cannot set its own rate and has no automatic share of the money, so its influence runs entirely through the evidence it brings to NEMSA's local consultation, which has to happen before any levy starts. The Quays and the Newcastle–Gateshead visitor offer are where most is at stake. We hold no borough measure of overnight stays or accommodation stock, so today we would arrive at that consultation with nothing to argue from. V-O1

What should we do?

Build the overnight-stay and accommodation-stock baseline now. It does not need the consultation to exist first, and it takes longer to build than a consultation will run. This is a proposal for commissioning, carried from September.

29 September · Prime Minister's Office

AmberA National Care Service From the Next Parliament, Paid for by Changing the Triple Lock

What changed?

The Prime Minister announced that 'a National Care Service will be established in the next Parliament, providing free personal care for older people, based on need rather than ability to pay' (Prime Minister's Office, 29 September 2026). The Triple Lock is kept until April 2030 and then adjusted so the State Pension rises by at least inflation or 2.5%, with a link to earnings over time. The adjustment is estimated to reduce state pension spending by £15 billion a year by the end of the 2030s. The service 'will not cover bed and board, for which existing means-tested council contributions will remain'. Baroness Casey's commission will recommend how and when it is built up, with its report due in summer 2027.

Why it matters?

Adult social care is one of the largest pressures on council budgets, and the announcement changes who pays for personal care without yet saying how money flows to councils. Bed and board stays means-tested through the council, so the council's assessment role continues. Care work is also part of the borough's foundational economy, so the workforce capacity the release says has to be built is a local labour market question.

What should we do?

Nothing yet. The phasing and the funding route to councils are for the Casey report in summer 2027, and that is the point to read it.

2. Labour Market and Skills

16 and 27 September · DWP

RedThe Jobs Guarantee Widens to Young People on Health Benefits, and Still Has No North East Partner

What changed?

Eligible young people on health benefits 'will be offered a guaranteed job on a voluntary basis from week 13 after their Work Capability Assessment', with the government covering '100% of wage costs up to 25 hours a week for six months'. The expansion 'will start from April 2027' (DWP, 27 September 2026). The original scheme covers 18 to 24 year olds 'who have been on Universal Credit and looking for work for 18 months', and its national rollout across 25 areas 'will go live later in 2026' (DWP, Jobs Guarantee, updated 16 September 2026). No North East delivery partner is named.

Gateshead's claimant count for those aged 18-24 for August 2026 is 1,040, against 940 in August 2025 (NOMIS, Claimant count by sex and age, NM_162_1). The North East figure is 14,715 and England's 295,410. The count includes some people in work who claim Universal Credit. September's figures are due on 20 October.

Why it matters?

The 18-24 rise of 100 matches the borough's whole net rise over the year (5,000 to 5,100 for all ages 16+ on the same pull), with a rise among 25 to 49 year olds offset by a fall among the over 50s, and this is the largest single instrument aimed at it. The health expansion reaches young people who are out of the labour market on health grounds, which is a group the claimant count does not see. That gives Gateshead two cohorts with two start dates. The release quotes the interim finding that 45% of young people who are not in education, employment or training report a disability.

What should we do?

Size both cohorts before the national rollout starts. The long-duration group needs a Universal Credit duration cut on the work-search condition, and the health group needs young people on health benefits after a Work Capability Assessment. Ask NEMSA or the DWP district who the delivery partner is, because the partner list is not on gov.uk.

28 September to 26 October · DWP

RedA £60 Million Fund for New Work and Health Support Is Open to Councils Until 26 October

What changed?

The Pathways to Work Innovation Fund opened for bids on 28 September and was launched publicly on 1 October (DWP, 1 October 2026). It offers 'a share of £60 million overall to test bold new ways of supporting disabled people and people with health conditions to enter, stay and progress in work'. Applications close on 26 October 2026 (applicant bid pack, updated 29 September 2026).

Why it matters?

Economic inactivity driven by long-term sickness is one of the borough's structural weaknesses, and councils are eligible. A bid needs a proposal and a delivery route rather than a statement of need, and four weeks is short for a council to design, partner and submit.

What should we do?

Settle whether Gateshead bids, joins a consortium or passes, and record the decision either way. If Gateshead is involved, the evidence case on inactivity and health is ours to supply.

23 September · DWP, DBT and DHSC

AmberNEMSA Is Named to Copy a Shared Occupational Health Scheme for Small Firms

What changed?

The September Keep Britain Working update sets out progress on a Workplace Health System built with eleven regional Vanguards, listed in the release as including the 'North East Combined Mayoral Authority', the name as published (DWP, 23 September 2026). The full report describes the Hull Resilience Hub, a buyer's pool letting small firms buy occupational health at large-firm prices, as 'something we are looking to replicate in different formats in other places across the country including West Yorkshire Combined Authority and the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority' (Keep Britain Working, September 2026).

Why it matters?

Gateshead's business base is overwhelmingly micro and small firms, which are the least able to buy occupational health on their own. A shared scheme run through NEMSA is one of the few employer-side levers on sickness-related inactivity that reaches them. Being named for replication is not the same as being funded.

What should we do?

Ask NEMSA what the North East replication involves and when. No data work is needed yet.

28 September and 1 October · HM Treasury and DWP · Skills

Amber£2,000 per Young Apprentice Now, and Mayoral Apprenticeship Brokerage From Spring 2027

What changed?

Announced on 1 October and now in effect, a non-levy paying employer gets £2,000 for each new apprentice aged 16 to 24, paid in two instalments of £1,000 through the training provider with no application needed (DWP, 1 October 2026). The Chancellor extended apprenticeship brokerage funding 'to all 14 existing Mayoral Strategic Authorities with directly elected mayors, so they can help provide local apprenticeship services across England from Spring 2027', with '£100 million of additional funding across 2 years' (HM Treasury, 28 September 2026). The same release restores the Union Learning Fund at £15 million a year and uses the National Wealth Fund to support a further 130,000 jobs by 2030.

Why it matters?

The hiring payment lands on exactly the small firms that make up most of the borough's business base, and the Federation of Small Businesses, quoted in the release, says it can be stacked with other support worth up to £6,000. Brokerage puts NEMSA between young people and local employers, so how it reaches Gateshead firms is a NEMSA design decision. D-O2

What should we do?

Add the hiring payment to the business support message now. Ask NEMSA how brokerage will run in Gateshead and what data it will share, before the service is designed rather than after.

15 September · DCMS

AmberGateshead Was Shortlisted for Young Futures Hubs and Not Selected, While Three NEMSA Neighbours Were

What changed?

DCMS selected 26 local authorities to open 42 new Young Futures Hubs by March 2029 (Young Futures Hubs and Transformation Programme, updated 15 and 17 September 2026). The North East selections are Newcastle upon Tyne and Northumberland with two hubs each, and South Tyneside, Redcar and Cleveland and Middlesbrough with one each. County Durham is already an early adopter with two. The 26 were chosen from 50 shortlisted authorities on 'local need, combining data on crime, deprivation and community need, and mental health'. Gateshead was one of the 50, on the list the same page published before its September update (archived copy of 6 August 2026). A further selection 'for local authorities to participate in the Local Youth Transformation only' runs 'from early 2027 onwards', which funds youth service transformation without a hub.

Why it matters?

The programme's stated goals are 'improving mental health and wellbeing, increasing opportunities, and reducing vulnerability to crime', which is the prevention end of our 18 to 24 problem. Gateshead sits between Newcastle and South Tyneside, both selected. DCMS's own need measure put the borough on the shortlist, so the outcome turned on the expression of interest or on regional distribution, and the published pages do not say which.

What should we do?

Establish with youth services colleagues whether Gateshead submitted an expression of interest and, if it did, what the feedback was. The early 2027 round funds transformation only, so it is worth entering for the youth service, and our deprivation and youth claimant evidence is the case for it.

10 to 23 September · DfE and NEMSA · Skills

AmberNEMSA Proposes 1,638 New Post-16 Places, and the 14 Plus Reforms Get a Mayoral Forum

What changed?

NEMSA proposes £8.1 million for a Post-16 Sufficiency Capital Programme funding 14 projects across all seven council areas, 'expected to create at least 1,638 additional learner places by the 2027/28 and 2028/29 academic years', subject to cabinet approval (NEMSA, 21 September 2026). Gateshead College is among the named providers. The Education Secretary convened mayors, business leaders and providers at No.10 North on 10 September on vocational education from 14, work experience and 'the devolution of billions of pounds of funding for 16 to 19-year-olds', described as 'the first of regular gatherings' (DfE, 10 September 2026). Vocational GCSEs follow, 'available in some subjects from as early as September 2029' (DfE, 23 September 2026).

Why it matters?

On our own evidence Gateshead holds up well at 16 to 17 and is exposed at 18 to 24. Devolved 16 to 19 funding touches the age band that currently works, so a change there is a risk to manage as much as an opportunity, and the forum seats mayors rather than constituent councils. D-O2

What should we do?

Put the question of what devolved 16 to 19 funding would mean for Gateshead providers to NEMSA, and watch for the cabinet decision on the capital programme.

3. Local Business and Industry

1 October to 30 November · Business department

RedManufacturers Can Register for Cheaper Electricity Until 30 November

What changed?

Applications opened on 1 October for the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme, which 'will cut manufacturers' electricity costs by up to 25 percent'. Registrations close on 30 November for support from April 2027 (Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade, 1 October 2026). 'Only those applying for BICS this year will be able to access the additional year of payment', a lump sum equal to around a year's relief. The impact assessment estimates small and micro businesses are 98% of firms in the frontier sectors and 99% in the foundational ones. The consultation outcome on eligibility was published on 30 September.

Why it matters?

Nearly every eligible firm is small, which is the kind least likely to hear about a registration window on its own. A firm that misses 30 November loses the extra year's payment for good. L-O3

What should we do?

Pass the scheme to business support colleagues now. A count of Gateshead firms in the eligible industry codes from UK Business Counts would give them a target list. It would be an upper bound, because the intensity and site tests cannot be applied to it, and it is a proposal for commissioning.

22 September · NEMSA · Local business news

AmberBusiness North East Brings Business Support Into One Regional Framework From January 2027

What changed?

NEMSA unveiled Business North East, a framework bringing NEMSA and the seven councils into 'a simpler, more coordinated business support system', with '£25.9 million between January 2027 and March 2029' (NEMSA, 22 September 2026). A regional hub provides specialist advice, and 'seven local authority spokes will maintain strong local relationships with businesses'. It runs five programmes, named Inspire, Explore, Start, Succeed and Grow. NEMSA's Business Advisory Council chair is also stepping down (NEMSA, 15 September 2026).

Why it matters?

The council's business support becomes the Gateshead spoke of a regional model, so what the council's business support does from January is shaped by the framework. The Succeed and Grow programmes are where productivity and scale-up sit, which is the borough's weakest point on our own evidence.

What should we do?

Agree with business support colleagues what evidence the Gateshead spoke needs before the January launch. The mid-sized firm count proposed in September is the natural first piece, and it can now be built on the 2026 business counts.

4. High Streets, Housing and Place

25 September · MHCLG · High streets and placemaking

Amber£210 Million for High Streets, With Every Application Route Still to Come

What changed?

MHCLG announced a £210 million package, including a new £125 million Derelict Buildings Fund through which the government 'will support local authorities to transform these buildings', £65 million to help communities rescue buildings and businesses at risk of closure, and £20 million split between High Street Rental Auctions and a £10 million regional Co-operative Development Programme for mayors (MHCLG, 25 September 2026). The money is 'from MHCLG funding already earmarked for high street support and regeneration'. A 'full high streets strategy' follows 'later this year'. No prospectus or dates have been published. Separately, no consultation, draft order or legislation has been published for the measures announced on 11 August on vape shops, adult gaming centres and the 'aim to permit' duty. That announcement promised no consultation, and said adult gaming centres would need planning permission 'under proposals due to come into effect at the start of next year' (No.10, 11 August 2026). Our September edition described the package as awaiting consultation, which the announcement does not support.

Why it matters?

The Derelict Buildings Fund is aimed at councils, and vacant and derelict property in the local centres is exactly what our July high streets work described. Until a prospectus appears we cannot tell whether the money is competitive or allocated. V-O2 V-O3

What should we do?

Watch for the prospectus and the strategy, and have vacancy evidence ready when a route opens. The baseline of vape, betting and adult gaming premises in the local centres is still worth doing before any use-class change removes the counterfactual.

14 September to 12 October · MHCLG

RedCommon Ground Award Applications Close on 12 October

Applications for the Common Ground Award 2026 to 2027 opened on 14 September and the 'deadline for applications' is 12 October 2026, with outcomes in December and payment likely in January 2027 (MHCLG prospectus, updated 17 September 2026). It is capital funding for organisations promoting social cohesion, and consortium bids are allowed.

Eligibility sits with voluntary and community organisations, so the council's role is convening a consortium, and convening takes most of the window. The item has been open in our weekly editions since 15 September without a decision.

Decide whether Gateshead convenes a consortium. If not, close the item as a decision not to bid rather than letting the window close by default.

21 September to 2 November · MHCLG and Homes England

RedCouncil Housebuilding Support Fund Bids Close at 2pm on 2 November

The 2026 to 2027 bidding round of the Council Housebuilding Support Fund opened on 21 September and 'will close at 2pm on 2 November 2026', with further windows expected in April 2027 and April 2028 (How to apply, Homes England; Capacity to Build, updated 21 September 2026). The Place Delivery Agreement model points to Homes England investment in the North East being agreed with NEMSA in future.

Confirm with housing and development colleagues whether Gateshead is bidding, and offer the evidence if so.
